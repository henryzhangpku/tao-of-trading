# Book proposal — Slow Is Fast: The Tao of Trading · 慢就是快：交易道德经

> **Superseded (20 November 2026).** This is the first proposal, which framed the book around quant research: backtests, forward tests, frozen rules and the AI desk. Henry redirected it to a mindset book: everything traders think they know, turned over by Laozi, with one habit to try per chapter. *Henry Zhang · 张恒 — proposal draft, 15 November 2026. Public from 17 November 2026; chapters appear as they are finished.*

## In one paragraph

Laozi wrote eighty-one short chapters on how to act without forcing, how the soft outlasts the hard, and why the one who knows when to stop is never in danger. This book follows him chapter by chapter, in his order: eighty-one short chapters for traders. Each one pairs a single verse of the Tao Te Ching with a single trading lesson and a single story from the author's own trading and research life — building Full Self Trading, running forward tests on frozen rules, letting the model propose and the rules decide. Stories, not lectures. It is the third book in the same shape as *The Art of Options War* (Sun Tzu, thirteen chapters) and sits beside it the way the *Tao Te Ching* sits beside *The Art of War*: the general tells you how to fight; the old man tells you when not to.

**Testing "Slow Is Fast."** It holds up as the title, for three reasons. It is the book's argument in three words, and the argument is true in the arithmetic: the trader who hurries trades more, pays more costs, sizes up after losses, abandons tests before they finish, and ends up back at the start — which is the slowest possible outcome. It is Laozi's shape of thought (the reversal: 反者道之动, "returning is the motion of the Tao," chapter 40) without pretending to be his words. And it is bilingual without strain: 慢就是快 is how a Chinese reader would say it at a dinner table.

One honesty note for the jacket copy. The line itself is not in the Tao Te Ching. Its closest relatives are 企者不立，跨者不行 ("who stands on tiptoe does not stand firm; who strides does not walk," chapter 24), 大器晚成 ("the great vessel is finished late," chapter 41), 图难于其易 ("plan the hard while it is easy," chapter 63) and 千里之行，始于足下 (chapter 64). The famous 欲速则不达 ("haste does not arrive") is Confucius, *Analects* 13.17, not Laozi. The book should say this once, in the prologue, and then use "slow is fast" as the author's own line distilled from Laozi — which is what it is.

## The hook — why traders will buy it

Every trader already knows what to do. The problem is doing it on the day. Trading-psychology books try to fix that with more instruction. Laozi does it with less: a few lines a day, each one an image you can hold in your head at 9:29 in the morning.

The old text maps onto the trader's real problems almost without translation:

- **Wu wei, 无为 — not forcing.** The market does not owe you a setup because you have time this morning. Most losses are trades that were forced: entered from boredom, from revenge, from a need to be busy. Doing nothing is a position, and usually the best one.
- **The soft overcomes the hard, 柔弱胜刚强.** Water wins by yielding. The rigid trader who must be right breaks; the one who takes the small loss and bends survives to be right later. Small size, defined risk and the stop are the soft things that outlast every hard conviction.
- **Knowing when to stop, 知止不殆.** The daily loss limit, the time stop, the trade not taken after the third loss, the strategy retired when it stops working. Knowing when to stop is the whole of risk management in four characters.
- **Emptiness and the uncarved block, 虚 and 朴.** The bowl is useful because it is empty; cash is a position. The uncarved block is the plain rule before it is decorated with filters fitted to last month. Every filter you add to a backtest carves the block — and makes it fit the past better and the future worse.
- **The model proposes, the rules decide.** The modern form of the sage who "acts without acting": an AI or a screen can suggest anything, but a written, frozen rule decides size, stop and exit, and nobody — including the author — edits it mid-test. The book makes this the spine of the author's stories.
- **Slow is fast.** The forward test that runs a full season before a single rule is touched. The trader who skips a hundred mediocre trades to take ten good ones. The account that compounds because it never had a fatal week.

Why buy *this* one: it is short (eighty-one chapters, one a day, under three months), bilingual, written by someone who builds and tests trading systems rather than someone who sells a course, and it makes no forecasts and no performance claims. It is the book you keep on the desk, not the one you read once.

## Audience

1. **Active retail traders** — options, stocks, futures, crypto — who know the mechanics and keep breaking their own rules. The core buyer.
2. **Systematic and quant-curious traders** who build backtests and want a language for overfitting, patience and leaving a rule alone. The forward-test and frozen-rule stories are written for them.
3. **Chinese-speaking traders**, in China and abroad, for whom 道德经 is a shared school text. The Chinese edition is written for them, not translated for them.
4. **Readers of the author's other books** — *The Art of Options War*, *The Options Underwriter*, *The Counted Trade* — and listeners on the Henry Zhang Books app.
5. Secondary: readers of philosophy who want a practical, non-mystical reading of Laozi.

## Format — mirroring *The Art of Options War*

Same repository shape as `art-of-options-war`: `README.md` (the book's front door), `LICENSE` (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0), `cover.jpg`, `en/` and `zh/` each with `README.md` (contents), `prologue.md`, `chapter-01.md` … `chapter-81.md` and `the-whole-book.md`. Free to read, typeset PDF and EPUB built from the same text, and the same route into the Henry Zhang Books app (a `content-tao-of-trading` shelf in `tianjin-in-california`, chapters generated from the app edition so the repo and the app cannot disagree) and into the daily book shorts (a new `books/catalog.py` shelf entry with its own permanent offset, added only when the first chapters are final).

Each chapter has four parts, the way *The Art of Options War* has The Seller, The Buyer and the Commentary:

1. **The Verse** — the Chinese original (received Wang Bi text) and an English translation. The translation is the author's own unless marked; where a line is borrowed it is from James Legge (1891), which is public domain. Every chapter says which.
2. **The Trader** — numbered short lines reading the verse for a trader, in Laozi's compressed manner.
3. **The Story** — one story from the author's trading and research life, told as a story: a morning, a decision, a rule. No lecture, no moral spelled out twice.
4. A closing line and the navigation to the next chapter.

Length: most chapters 800–1,500 words; a handful of central ones (1, 8, 24, 33, 40, 44, 64, 76, 78) up to 2,500. The whole book roughly 90,000–110,000 words in English.

## Chapter outline — all eighty-one, in Laozi's order

Each line: chapter · title · opening of the verse · the trading lesson · the story. Stories are seeds drawn from the systems and records the author built; each is checked against his own notes and logs before drafting, and any seed he cannot confirm is replaced rather than embellished.

2. **Long and Short Make Each Other** 《天下皆知美之为美》 — having and not having produce each other · a win rate means nothing without the payoff beside it · Story: writing the breakeven win rate on the ticket for the first time, and seeing a "great" strategy turn ordinary.
3. **Do Not Display the Desirable** 《不尚贤》 — not showing what is coveted keeps the heart still · hide the running P&L, show the rules · Story: deciding what a trading app's screen should and should not show the person holding the position.
4. **The Empty Vessel** 《道冲》 — the Tao is empty and never filled · cash is a position; unused buying power is capacity, not waste · Story: a week of no entries, and why it was the right week.
5. **Straw Dogs** 《天地不仁》 — heaven and earth are not kind · the market does not know you are there · Story: a perfect setup that failed for no reason the logs could show, and leaving it at that.
6. **The Valley Spirit** 《谷神不死》 — the valley spirit never dies · stay low and liquid and you survive every season · Story: why the systems refuse thin markets on entry — the valley is where the water still flows.
7. **Heaven Lasts Long** 《天长地久》 — it lasts because it does not live for itself · the strategy that does not need to win today is the one still running next year · Story: choosing to judge rules over seasons, not days.

## Voice notes

- **Henry's voice, from *The Art of Options War*.** Short declarative sentences. Plain words. Arithmetic you can check on paper. Dry, occasionally wry, never hyped. The trader is "you" in The Trader; the stories are "I."
- **Stories, not lectures.** Each story is a scene: a time of day, a screen, a decision, one sentence of what was learned — and then stop. The verse does the moralising so the story does not have to.
- **Laozi's compression.** The Trader section is numbered lines, each sayable in a breath, the way the original is. No bullet-point listicles.
- **Honest about the source.** Quote the Chinese, translate it honestly, mark whose translation it is (the author's own, or Legge 1891 for marked lines). Never put modern words in Laozi's mouth; "slow is fast" is the author's line.
- **Not mystical.** No qi, no fate, no "the universe wants you to win." The Tao here is the arithmetic of survival and the discipline of leaving a rule alone.
- **Hard rules.** Education, not advice: no recommendations, signals, forecasts or price targets. No performance claims: no returns, win rates or P&L of the author's or anyone's trades, real or paper; where a number appears it is arithmetic the reader can check. Employers and career moves are never named. Systems are described by what they do (frozen rules, forward tests, the gate), not by results.

**English edition** — written first, chapter by chapter, Part I then Part II. Open edition on GitHub (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, free), typeset PDF and EPUB built from the same text, the Henry Zhang Books app with audiobook narration, and the Quant Signals shop as a free download.

**Chinese edition · 中文版** — written by hand, not machine-translated, the same way *The Art of Options War* was written twice. Each chapter quotes the original 道德经 text in full (no translation needed), so the Chinese edition is the closer reading: The Trader section can play on the original's wording (知止, 无为, 守柔, 慎终如始) in a way English cannot. Simplified characters first; a Traditional edition can be generated from it for Taiwan, Hong Kong and overseas readers. Title: 慢就是快：交易道德经.
