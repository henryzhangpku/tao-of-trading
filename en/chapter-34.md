# 34. The Great Tao Flows Everywhere

*Part I: The Way · 《大道泛兮》*

## The Verse

大道泛兮，其可左右。

万物恃之而生而不辞，功成不名有。衣养万物而不为主，常无欲，可名于小；万物归焉而不为主，可名为大。

以其终不自为大，故能成其大。

The great way flows everywhere. It can go to the left or to the right.

The ten thousand things rely on it for life, and it does not turn them away. When the work is done, it does not claim it. It clothes and feeds the ten thousand things and does not act as their master. Always without desire, it can be called small. The ten thousand things return to it and it does not act as their master, so it can be called great.

Because it never makes itself great, it can become great.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## The Trader

1. A system that works flows everywhere and does not need watching. If it needs you every hour, it is not yet a system.

2. It can go left or right: long, short or flat, as the rule finds. It does not need you to have a side.

3. The things rely on it and it does not turn them away. Every candidate gets the same checks, the dull ones and the exciting ones alike.

4. When the work is done, it does not claim it. Do not take credit for the rule's good weeks. You did not make them. You only did not get in the way.

5. Do not take the blame for its bad weeks either, unless you touched it. If you touched it, the week is yours.

6. It does not act as master. The scheduled run runs. Read the log afterwards, not the screen during.

7. Always without desire, it can be called small. A good system has no wish to trade. It trades when the rule says so, and otherwise it waits.

8. The first week you let it run without checking every hour is the week you learn whether you built a system or a habit of watching.

9. Because it never makes itself great, it becomes great: small rules, run every day, without your name on them.

## The Story

When the first scheduled runs went live, I watched them the way new parents watch a sleeping baby. The night's work runs on a machine in the cloud: data in, rules applied, the forward books filled at the day's prices, everything written to a log. I had built it so that I would not have to be there. Then I was there for all of it.

I kept a window open on my desk that showed the log as it was written, line by line. During the session I checked the board every hour, sometimes more. I told myself this was diligence. A system that trades on rules should be watched until you trust it. That sounds right, and it has a flaw. Watching does not produce trust. It produces more watching.

---

One Sunday I wrote down what the watching had actually done that week. Not what it had felt like. What it had done.

The answer was nothing, with one exception, and the exception was worse than nothing. On the Wednesday I had seen a position slip in the middle of the day and opened its card to look at the stop, as if looking would hold it up. On the Thursday I had moved a resting order on one name by a small amount, because the price was close to it and I had a feeling. That was in a paper book that was not frozen, so it cost nothing but the record. But the record was the whole point of the book. For the rest of that season that one order was not the rule's; it was mine, and I could no longer say what the rule alone would have done there.

The fifth of the lines above came from that Thursday.

---

So I made a plan for one week and wrote it on a card. I would read the log twice a day: once before five in the morning, when the night's runs finish, and once after the close. Not the board during the session. Not the live window. If something needed me in between, the system would have to say so.

That last sentence was the real work, and it took most of a weekend. I had been watching because I did not know what failure would look like, so now I had to decide. A run that does not start. A run that starts and does not finish. Prices that arrive late, or stale. A book whose positions do not match what the executor says it holds. For each, a check that runs on its own, and a message to my phone if it fails. Not a message for a trade, or a loss, or a big move. Only for a broken thing. A trade is the system working. It does not need me.

Then I closed the live window, and on Monday I did not open it.

---

The first two days were uncomfortable in a way I had not expected. Not anxious, exactly. Idle. My hands kept looking for the window. Twice I opened the board out of habit and shut it again before it loaded, which felt foolish and slightly heroic in about equal measure.

On the Wednesday night something did break. A data source was late, and the night's run reached the step where it fills the books before the prices it needed had arrived. I was asleep. The run did what I had written for that case a few weekends earlier: it refused to fill anything on stale prices, wrote the refusal and its reason to the log, waited, and tried again. The second try found the prices and finished. My phone had one message, which I read in the morning, followed by a second saying the run had completed.

I read the whole thing before five, with coffee, as an account of the night. It was the most reassuring thing the system had done since I built it. Not because nothing went wrong. Because something went wrong and I was not needed. *When the work is done, it does not claim it.* The log said nothing about its own cleverness. It said what happened, in order, and stopped.

---

By Friday the watching had a different shape in my mind. I had thought of it as care. It was closer to a vote of no confidence, cast every hour, in a system I had built precisely so that I would not have to cast it. And every hour of watching was an hour spent close enough to the controls to touch them, which is the only way I have ever spoiled a record.

I kept the plan. It is not a week any more; it is how the desk runs. The log in the morning, the log after the close, and a phone that stays silent unless something is broken. On most days it is silent.

*It clothes and feeds the ten thousand things and does not act as their master.* I used to think that line was about the Tao being modest. Now I hear it as a specification. A good system feeds the books, keeps the record, and does not need an owner standing over it. *Because it never makes itself great, it can become great.* Nor does it need me to make it feel important by watching. That was a habit, and the habit was mine.
