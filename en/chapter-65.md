# 65. The Ancients Did Not Enlighten

*Part II: The Virtue · 《古之善为道者》*

## The Verse

古之善为道者，非以明民，将以愚之。民之难治，以其智多。

故以智治国，国之贼；不以智治国，国之福。

知此两者亦稽式。常知稽式，是谓玄德。玄德深矣，远矣，与物反矣，然后乃至大顺。

The ancients who were good at the way did not use it to make the people clever. They used it to keep them plain. People are hard to govern because they have too much cleverness.

So to govern a state by cleverness is to rob it. Not to govern it by cleverness is its good fortune.

To know these two is also to know the pattern. Always to know the pattern is called dark virtue. Dark virtue is deep, and far, and turns back against things, and only then reaches the great accord.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## The Trader

1. Keep the rule plain. The ancients did not make the people clever, and a trader should not make his rules clever either.

2. A strategy too clever to explain is too clever to trust. If you cannot say in one sentence why it should work, you do not know what you are holding.

3. An account is hard to govern when it has too much cleverness in it: a filter for every loss, an exception for every regime, a special case for every name that ever hurt.

4. Each clever fix solves one past problem and adds one future surprise. That is what it means to rob the state.

5. Not governing by cleverness is good fortune. A plain rule fails in plain ways, and plain failures can be read.

6. Here is the pattern to keep: say the reason before you show the chart. If the reason needs the chart to sound true, it is the chart talking.

7. I have dropped ideas that passed their tests because I could not explain them to myself in one sentence. I do not miss them.

8. Dark virtue turns back against things. Research grows by adding. Plainness grows by taking away, and that runs against every instinct of a person who likes to build.

9. Only then the great accord. A plain rule does not fit the market. It goes along with it.
