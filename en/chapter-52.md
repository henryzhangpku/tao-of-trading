# 52. Hold to the Mother

*Part II: The Virtue · 《天下有始》*

## The Verse

天下有始，以为天下母。既得其母，以知其子；既知其子，复守其母，没身不殆。

塞其兑，闭其门，终身不勤。开其兑，济其事，终身不救。

见小曰明，守柔曰强。用其光，复归其明，无遗身殃；是为习常。

The world had a beginning, and it can be taken as the mother of the world. Having found the mother, you know the children. Knowing the children, go back and keep to the mother, and to the end of your life you are not in danger.

Block the openings, shut the doors, and to the end of your life you will not be worn out. Open the openings, add to your affairs, and to the end of your life there is no saving you.

Seeing the small is called clarity. Keeping to the soft is called strength. Use the light, return to the clarity, and leave yourself no calamity. This is called following the constant.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## What You Know

Stay reachable. You grew up with a phone in your pocket and learned what everyone learns now: answer fast, keep up, never be the last to hear. At work, the one who replies at ten at night is the one who gets trusted. In the market it matters even more, because the market never really closes. Something is always trading somewhere. So a serious trader has the app on his phone, alerts on every level, the group chat open, the futures checked last thing at night and first thing in the morning. Miss the headline and you miss the move. The trader who is always on is never caught out.

## The Other Way

1. *Block the openings, shut the doors, and to the end of your life you will not be worn out.* Every open door is a door something can walk through. An alert, a message, a headline: each one walks in and asks you to do something.

2. *Open the openings, add to your affairs, and there is no saving you.* The trader who can be reached by everything is moved by everything. He is not informed. He is pushed.

3. Most of what reaches you outside your hours is something you cannot act on, or should not. Knowing it at dinner changes nothing except dinner.

4. The phone in your pocket is a trading desk you carry to bed. A desk is for sitting at, and then for leaving.

5. *The world had a beginning, and it can be taken as the mother.* Every trade comes from somewhere: your reasons, your plan, the thinking you did while you were calm. That is the mother. The ticks are her children.

6. *Knowing the children, go back and keep to the mother.* When the screen confuses you, do not stare harder at the screen. Go back to what you wrote before it opened.

7. *Seeing the small is called clarity.* Small things are seen only by a quiet mind: a slight change in how a name trades, the first sign that you are tired. A mind full of alerts sees only what is loud.

8. *Keeping to the soft is called strength.* The rested trader bends with a bad morning. The worn-out one snaps at it.

9. *Use the light, return to the clarity.* Look at the market when it is your time to look. Then close it and look at something else. Both halves are the work.

10. *This is called following the constant.* The same hours and the same shutting of the doors, every day. That sameness is what lets the unusual day stand out.

## The Story

It is Thursday evening and you hold two positions overnight. At a quarter past seven, halfway through dinner, the phone buzzes on the table. A price alert: one of your names is down one and a half percent after hours, because a company in the same business has just reported. You pick up the phone. You are still holding the fork.

The group chat already knows. Someone says it will gap down hard tomorrow. Someone else says it is a buying chance. You read both, then read the report, then read what people are saying about the report. Somebody at the table asks you a question and you answer the wrong one.

At half past ten you check the futures. They are a little lower. You check again before you sleep. You wake at two and reach for the phone without deciding to. By six you have read forty posts about a stock you could not trade for three and a half more hours.

---

At the open you are tired before the first tick. The name gaps down, as everyone said, and you sell it in the first minute, at almost the low of the day, because you have been carrying this fear since dinner and you want it gone. By half past ten it has climbed back to where it closed yesterday. You also bought a small company someone in the chat was excited about at six in the morning. You do not remember deciding to.

The other position, the one that had no news, is quietly doing something it has not done before. It is failing to bounce when the market bounces. It is a small thing. You do not see it. Your eyes are worn out from a night of looking at loud things.

By the afternoon you are short-tempered and slow, and every new alert lands on a mind with nothing left to meet it. *Open the openings, add to your affairs.* You opened every one of them, and the day had you before it began.

---

Now the other Thursday.

At half past four you close the platform. Before you do, you write one line for each position: what would make you act tomorrow. For the first name, if it trades below yesterday's low after the first half hour, you are out. Then you shut the doors. The trading app is not on your phone. The chat is muted until the morning.

Dinner is dinner. You hear about the other company's report the next morning at half past eight, when you open the doors at the time you always open them. The name is down before the bell. You read your line before you read anything else. Then you read the news.

It opens lower and holds above yesterday's low. Your line says nothing to do, so you do nothing. You are rested, and that lets you see something small: the second position is not bouncing with the market. It has not broken anything yet. But it has started to trade heavily, and you planned for that, so you take off half, as you wrote you would.

At half past four you write tomorrow's lines and close the doors again.

---

*Block the openings, shut the doors, and to the end of your life you will not be worn out.* We are taught to be reachable, to answer quickly, to be the one who always knows. The old book sees it the other way round. Every opening you leave open lets something in to spend you. Keep to the mother, the plan you made while you were calm, and close the rest.

The news that came at dinner was still there at half past eight. It had not changed, and neither had your line.

## Try This

For the next five sessions, choose a time to close your trading and a time to open it, and keep every door shut in between: no alerts on the phone, no platform, no chat, no futures. If a position needs protecting overnight, put the order in before you close. Write one line per position about what would make you act tomorrow, and read it first in the morning. At the end of the week, look at what you missed overnight, and whether knowing it earlier would have changed anything you did.
