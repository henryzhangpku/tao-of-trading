# 52. Hold to the Mother

*Part II: The Virtue · 《天下有始》*

## The Verse

天下有始，以为天下母。既得其母，以知其子；既知其子，复守其母，没身不殆。

塞其兑，闭其门，终身不勤。开其兑，济其事，终身不救。

见小曰明，守柔曰强。用其光，复归其明，无遗身殃；是为习常。

The world had a beginning, and it can be taken as the mother of the world. Having found the mother, you know the children. Knowing the children, go back and keep to the mother, and to the end of your life you are not in danger.

Block the openings, shut the doors, and to the end of your life you will not be worn out. Open the openings, add to your affairs, and to the end of your life there is no saving you.

Seeing the small is called clarity. Keeping to the soft is called strength. Use the light, return to the clarity, and leave yourself no calamity. This is called following the constant.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## The Trader

1. The mother is the rule. The children are the trades. Know one rule well and every trade it gives you can be read.

2. When a trade confuses you, go back to the mother. Not "what does the chart say now", but "what did the rule say, and has it changed its mind".

3. Block the openings, shut the doors: the alerts, the second screen, the group chat, the feed that refreshes by itself.

4. Keep quiet hours. There are times when nothing is sent and no one is woken. A system that trades for you should not wake you for things you cannot act on, and most things are things you cannot act on.

5. Open every opening and there is no saving you. The trader who can be reached by every alert is moved by every alert.

6. Seeing the small is clarity: the cost that creeps up, the fill that is a little worse each week, the rule that has drifted by one word. Only someone not busy with loud things sees small things early.

7. Keeping to the soft is strength. Small size gives you room to be wrong slowly, which is the only way to learn anything.

8. Use the light, then return to the clarity. Look at the screen when the rule calls you. Then look away.

9. Following the constant: the same routine every day. It is what makes the strange day stand out.

## The Story

The message that changed things arrived at twenty past three in the morning. My phone lit up on the bedside table and buzzed against the wood, and I was awake before I knew why. I read it with one eye open. A paper book had filled an order on a market on the other side of the world. It was paper. There was no money in it. Nothing about the fill was wrong. The system had simply done what it was built to do, and then, because I had built it that way, it had told me.

I lay there for an hour. Not worrying, exactly. Just awake, with the screen's light still behind my eyes, thinking about a fill I could do nothing about and did not need to.

---

The next afternoon I printed out every kind of message the desk could send me, and there were more than I expected. Fills, on every book, real and paper. Stops placed. Stops moved by a trailing rule. Entries refused, with the reason. Feeds late. Feeds recovered. A job finished. A job finished slowly. The night's research run done. Each one had been added on some day for some reason, usually the day I built the thing it reported on, when I was proud of it and wanted to watch it work.

Beside each line I wrote one question: *if this arrives, is there anything I must do, and can only I do it?*

Almost every line failed. A fill on a frozen book is not a thing to act on; acting on it is the one thing forbidden. A refused entry is the rule doing its work. A stop moved by a rule is the rule. A late feed recovers by itself or it does not, and either way the system already knows what to do: refuse new entries on stale data, never block an exit. The night's run finishing is a fact I can learn at five, when I get up, as well as at three.

*Block the openings, shut the doors, and to the end of your life you will not be worn out.* I had been holding every door open, and calling it being in control.

---

What came out of that afternoon was a set of quiet hours. They cover the night and a few other stretches of the day: dinner, and the hour before sleep. During them, nothing is sent and no one is woken. The system does not stop. It runs in the cloud on its written rules whether I am awake or not, and exits are never held for my attention. The only thing that changes is that it keeps its news to itself. Everything it would have said goes into one note, which is waiting when I get up.

There is one exception, and it took me a long time to write, because I wanted it to be very narrow. The quiet hours break only for something that is both urgent and mine: a fault that the system cannot handle by its own rules and that will cost something real if nobody acts before morning. In practice that is almost never. The exception has a test of its own, and I check it once a month to make sure it still works, because a door you never open can rust shut.

The first weeks were strange. I would wake at four from habit and reach for the phone, and it would be dark, and I would lie there not knowing whether that meant nothing had happened or that something had and was waiting. After a while I stopped reaching. Then I stopped waking.

---

Here is the part I did not expect.

With the alerts gone, the morning note became the only place I met the night, and I read it slowly, the whole thing, with the coffee, before five. The same note every morning, in the same order. *Following the constant.* Because it was the same every morning, small differences in it began to show, the way you notice a picture that has been moved an inch on a wall you see every day.

After a few weeks I noticed that the fills on one of the paper books were a little worse than the prices the rule had asked for. Not much. A little, and then a little more, week by week. In the stream of alerts, each fill had arrived on its own, in the middle of the night, one at a time, and each one had looked fine. Laid side by side in one note, they made a slope.

I followed it back. The cause was dull: a change in how one data source stamped its times had made the book's orders go out a little later than the rule meant them to. The book had been paying a toll that grew every week, and no single message had ever been large enough to see.

*Seeing the small is called clarity.* I would never have seen it in the noise. Only someone not busy with loud things sees small things early, and for years I had made sure I was always busy with loud things. The fix to the timing went through the ledger like any change, with its own fingerprint and a day count starting at one, and the paper book under the old fingerprint ran on to the end of its season exactly as it was, toll and all, because that is what frozen means.

---

*Keeping to the soft is called strength.* The soft thing in this story is a phone that stays dark. It feels like less control. It is the opposite. The trader who can be reached by every alert is moved by every alert. The one who sleeps reads the whole night at once, in order, and sees its shape.

I still get up before five. Now I get up to read, not because something woke me.
