# 51. The Tao Gives Birth

*Part II: The Virtue · 《道生之，德畜之》*

## The Verse

道生之，德畜之，物形之，势成之。是以万物莫不尊道而贵德。

道之尊，德之贵，夫莫之命而常自然。

故道生之，德畜之；长之育之；亭之毒之；养之覆之。生而不有，为而不恃，长而不宰，是谓玄德。

The way gives them birth. Virtue rears them. Things give them shape. Circumstance completes them. So all the ten thousand things revere the way and honour virtue.

The way is revered and virtue honoured not because anyone commands it, but always of themselves.

So the way gives birth and virtue rears; it grows them and raises them, settles them and ripens them, feeds them and shelters them. It gives life and does not possess. It acts and does not lean on what it does. It leads and does not rule. This is called the dark virtue.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## The Trader

1. You give the rule birth. Then it has to grow by itself: through the data, through the test, through the forward run.

2. Circumstance completes it. The market decides in which seasons a rule is paid. You do not.

3. Gives life and does not possess. A rule you love is a rule you will protect from the evidence.

4. Let a new rule live in the shadow first, running on paper beside the real ones, where being wrong costs nothing but pride.

5. Acts and does not lean on it. A rule's good month is not to your credit. Its bad month is not your disgrace.

6. Leads and does not rule. Set the walls before it starts: the size, the stop, the length of the test. Inside the walls, do not steer.

7. Settles them and ripens them. A rule pulled out of the shadow early is fruit picked green. It looks the same and tastes of nothing.

8. Feeds them and shelters them. Keep its data clean, keep its costs honest, and keep everyone's hands off it, your own first.

9. No one commands it. A rule that only works while you watch it is not a rule yet.

10. The dark virtue: the best systems look, from outside, as if nobody built them.

## The Story

I gave the rule a name. That was the first mistake, and I did not know it was one until much later.

It was a good name, a small private joke, the kind you give a thing you have spent a long time making. The rule had taken most of a winter. It had come through the gate cleanly: after costs, after twice the costs, in both halves of the history, with its best month removed. I was fond of it in a way I had not been fond of a rule for years. When it was ready, I called it by its name in my notes, and I said the name out loud when I talked about it, and I could feel, already, that I would be disappointed on its behalf if it failed.

---

It went into shadow, which is where every new rule on my desk goes first. Shadow means it runs beside the real books on the same board, on the same prices, every day, and nothing it says touches an order. It is paper, with no money at stake. Before it starts, three walls are set and written down: the size it would trade, the stops it would use, and the length of its shadow season. The season cannot be shortened because the rule is doing well, or lengthened because it is doing badly. The walls go up first so that I am not tempted to move them later.

Its first weeks in shadow were bad. Not dramatic, just plainly wrong in the way new rules often are: it bought a few names that fell at once, it stopped out of one that turned the next day, and on the board its line sat below the line of its random control, the book of dice-chosen picks that every rule is measured against.

I noticed that I had started writing in the margin.

The shadow log has a column for notes, and I had always kept it for facts: a feed that came in late, a holiday, a price that had to be corrected. Now I was writing other things. *Odd week for this kind of name. Report season distorting the signal. Should recover once the market settles.* Each note was plausible. Each one, read in order, was an excuse. I was standing beside the rule on the board like a parent at a school sports day, explaining to anyone who would listen why my child had not really lost the race.

*It gives life and does not possess.* I had given the rule life, and I was possessing it in the most ordinary way there is: by refusing to let it be wrong without me.

---

Two things changed, and both were small.

The first was the notes column. It is back to facts, and the desk now enforces it. A note in a shadow log may say what happened to the data. It may not say what the rule meant, or what should happen next. The model on the desk reads the logs each morning and flags any note that explains a result rather than recording a fact. It flagged several of mine that week, politely, and it was right each time.

The second was the name. I took it off. On my desk now, a rule in shadow is known by its fingerprint, the short string computed from its file. It is not a name anyone could love. That is the point. You do not make excuses for a string of letters and numbers. You read what it did.

I found this harder than it sounds. A fingerprint is unpleasant to say. I caught myself, for a while, using the old name in my head. But after a few weeks the rule began to look different on the board. Not better, not worse. Just less like mine. It looked like a thing that was growing by itself, through the data and the season, the way the chapter says a rule should, while I kept the walls in place and my hands off it.

---

*It acts and does not lean on what it does. It leads and does not rule.* The shadow season ran its length. I did not shorten it when the rule recovered, which it did for a while, and I did not lengthen it when it slipped again. At the end it was judged the way every rule is judged, against its random twin, over the whole season, by a test written before it started. What the result was is the rule's business and the ledger's, and I am not going to tell you. The whole point of shadow is that being wrong there is private.

What I can tell you is what being wrong in private feels like when you let it. It feels like nothing. A bad week in shadow costs a little pride, and the pride is the only thing that wants to stop the test. Take the name off, keep the margin clean, and even the pride has very little to hold on to.

I think of Laozi's line about the dark virtue now whenever I look at the board. *The best systems look, from outside, as if nobody built them.* That is not quite his sentence; it is mine, in the Trader lines of this chapter. His is shorter: the way gives birth, virtue rears, and it gives life and does not possess. Someone built every rule on that board. The ones I trust most are the ones in which I can no longer find myself.
