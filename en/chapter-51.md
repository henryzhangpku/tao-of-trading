# 51. The Tao Gives Birth

*Part II: The Virtue · 《道生之，德畜之》*

## The Verse

道生之，德畜之，物形之，势成之。是以万物莫不尊道而贵德。

道之尊，德之贵，夫莫之命而常自然。

故道生之，德畜之；长之育之；亭之毒之；养之覆之。生而不有，为而不恃，长而不宰，是谓玄德。

The way gives them birth. Virtue rears them. Things give them shape. Circumstance completes them. So all the ten thousand things revere the way and honour virtue.

The way is revered and virtue honoured not because anyone commands it, but always of themselves.

So the way gives birth and virtue rears; it grows them and raises them, settles them and ripens them, feeds them and shelters them. It gives life and does not possess. It acts and does not lean on what it does. It leads and does not rule. This is called the dark virtue.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## What You Know

Do your own work. School marked down the copied answer and praised the one you worked out yourself. At work, the person with the original idea is the one people remember. Trading rewards this more than anything. Tips are everywhere and most are worth nothing. The best trades are the ones you find yourself: a detail in a report nobody read, a business you understand better than the crowd, a move you saw coming before anyone wrote about it. An idea you dug up, checked and believed in before anyone else is worth more than any tip. When you have one, you should back it.

## The Other Way

1. *The way gives them birth. Virtue rears them. Things give them shape. Circumstance completes them.* You can give birth to an idea. You cannot finish it. Once it is in the market, the market shapes it and the market ends it.

2. Digging up an idea yourself is good work. It is still only the first of four hands on the trade.

3. *It gives life and does not possess.* The idea you authored is the one you hold longest when it is wrong. You know every reason it should work, and you are fond of each of them.

4. A borrowed idea you cut without a second thought. Your own idea you explain. Same price, same stop, two different traders, and both of them are you.

5. Research does not make you right. It makes you attached. The hours you spent are paid into the position, and you want them back.

6. *It acts and does not lean on what it does.* Do not lean on the work behind the idea. The price does not know how long you read.

7. "Before anyone else" sounds like an edge. It also describes most ideas that never worked. Being early and being wrong look the same for a long time.

8. *It leads and does not rule.* Write the exit before you enter, and write it as if a stranger had handed you the idea.

9. *Not because anyone commands it, but always of themselves.* The market does not ask who found the trade. Your discovery and a stranger's tip get the same fill and the same loss.

10. *This is called the dark virtue.* Do the digging, then let the idea go out into the market as if it had no parent. Nobody will see you do it. That is the point.

## The Story

You have been on this one for three weeks. A small supplier, a footnote in its last report that nobody in any forum has mentioned, an order book you pieced together from two customers' filings. You read late into the evenings. By Wednesday you are sure. Nobody gave you this. You found it.

On Thursday morning you buy at 18.40, at more size than usual, because you know this one better than anything you have ever traded. The stop goes at 17.60. The same morning you also take a smaller trade on a name a colleague mentioned in passing, at your normal size.

By ten both are down. The colleague's name hits its stop at 9:55 and you are out without a thought; it was only a tip. Yours trades at 17.90, then 17.70. You tell yourself the market has not noticed the footnote yet. That is the whole point of finding it first.

---

At 17.60 you cancel the stop. The footnote is still true. Nothing has changed except the price, and the price is exactly what you expected the crowd to get wrong. By the following Tuesday it is at 16.20, and you buy more, because now it is cheaper and you are still the only one who knows.

Three weeks later you are still holding it. You have a paragraph ready for anyone who asks, and a longer one for yourself at night. The tip cost you a few dollars and you have forgotten it. Your own idea has cost you many times that, and you cannot stop thinking about it, because closing it would mean the three weeks were wasted and the footnote meant nothing.

*It gives life and does not possess.* You gave the idea life, and then you would not let it be anything but right.

---

Now the other Thursday.

The same three weeks, the same footnote, the same late evenings. Before you buy, you write the exit, and you write it the way you would for an idea someone texted you: 17.60, normal size. At the top of the note you write one word: mine. It is there to warn you.

By ten both trades are down. The colleague's name stops out at 9:55. Yours slips toward 17.60, and you feel your mind start to build the paragraph: the market has not noticed, the footnote is still true. You know that voice. It is the voice of a parent, not a trader.

At 17.60 the stop fills. You are out of your own idea exactly the way you were out of the borrowed one, for the same small loss.

A week later you look at the name again, with fresh eyes. Perhaps the footnote matters and the stock will set up again; if it does, that will be a new trade with a new exit. Perhaps it never matters. Either way, it is not costing you anything while you find out.

---

*It gives life and does not possess. It acts and does not lean on what it does.* We are taught that original work is worth more, and that what you made yourself deserves your loyalty. The old book sees it the other way round. Make the idea with all the care you have. Then let it go into the market, where it was never yours.

The idea was yours. The trade, once it was filled, was not.

## Try This

For the next ten trades, mark each idea "mine" or "borrowed" before you enter, and write the exit before you place the order. Keep the marks and the exits on one sheet. After ten trades, look at the losers in each group: how long you held them, and how often you moved the exit. If your own ideas were held longer and moved more, you have found where your loyalty is costing you.
