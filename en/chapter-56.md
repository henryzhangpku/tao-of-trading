# 56. Those Who Know Do Not Speak

*Part II: The Virtue · 《知者不言》*

## The Verse

知者不言，言者不知。

塞其兑，闭其门，挫其锐，解其纷，和其光，同其尘，是谓玄同。

故不可得而亲，不可得而疏；不可得而利，不可得而害；不可得而贵，不可得而贱。故为天下贵。

Those who know do not speak. Those who speak do not know.

Block the openings. Shut the doors. Blunt the sharp, untie the tangled, soften the glare, settle with the dust. This is called the dark sameness.

So he cannot be drawn close and cannot be pushed away. He cannot be helped and cannot be harmed. He cannot be honoured and cannot be shamed. That is why the world honours him.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## The Trader

1. The trader who talks about his positions is trading the conversation. Once you have said it out loud, getting out means taking it back.

2. A position you announce has an audience. An audience is a second stop, placed by other people, at the price where you would look foolish.

3. Those who speak do not know. The loudest calls are made by people who will not be counted against them.

4. Block the openings. Keep what you hold off the group chat, off the feed, off the dinner table.

5. Shut the doors: on the forum thread about your stock, on the video telling you that you are right. Agreement is not information.

6. Blunt the sharp, untie the tangled. This is the fourth chapter's line again, here pointed inward, at the urge to explain yourself.

7. Talk about the rule, if you have to talk. Never about the position. The rule can be argued with. The position should only be managed.

8. He cannot be drawn close and cannot be pushed away. Praise for a trade should move your size as little as mockery does.

9. He cannot be helped and cannot be harmed. A trader nobody is watching can leave at the stop without saving face.

10. Silence is not secrecy. It keeps one person out of the trade, and that person is the one who wants to be seen being right.

## The Story

For a while I belonged to a group chat of old classmates who traded. It was friendly and mostly harmless. Someone would post a chart in the morning, someone else would post a joke about it, and by the evening there would be a long thread of opinions on where everything was going.

I posted a position once. Only once, which is why I remember it so clearly. It was a swing trade, entered by the rule, with the stop written on the card before the order went out. I posted it because a friend asked what I was looking at, and because, if I am honest, I liked the setup and wanted someone to say so. Several people did. One of them said it was the best idea he had seen all week.

Two days later it was near the stop.

---

I want to describe what happened in my head, because it was new to me and it has not happened since.

On every other trade I had taken, the stop was a price. That morning the stop was a price and a message. If the trade went through the stop, I would sell, and then I would have to go back to the chat, where my position was still sitting in the thread under the words *best idea all week*, and say that I was out. Nobody would have been unkind about it. They were friends. That made no difference. I caught myself working out how I would phrase it before I had decided anything, and I noticed that I was hoping, very slightly, that the price would hold until after the close, so that I could deal with it tomorrow.

That hope was the second stop, the one other people had placed for me without meaning to. It sat at the price where I would look foolish, and it was pulling against the first one.

The rule sold at the stop. I had built it so that I could not hesitate on its behalf, and I did not. Then I wrote a short message in the chat saying I was out, someone sent a sympathetic face, and within minutes the conversation had moved on. The whole thing cost nothing the rule had not already agreed to. But I sat at the desk afterwards feeling as though I had been let off something. I had seen the second stop, and I had seen that it was not mine to control.

*Those who know do not speak. Those who speak do not know.*

---

The decision I made that evening was simple, and I have kept it: I do not say what I hold. Not in a chat, not at dinner, not online. Not the name, not the direction, not the size.

People sometimes find this odd, because so much of what I build is public. The desk keeps a record of what its rules do, and some of that record is shown openly, after the fact, as a record: the rule, the date, what it did. That is not the same thing, and the difference is the whole of this chapter. A record is published by the rule, according to the rule, and nobody's face is attached to any line of it. It cannot be proud of a trade, so it never has to take one back. What I keep quiet is the other thing: my own voice, while a position is open, telling anyone what I think of it.

*Block the openings. Shut the doors.* I left the group chat a little later. Not dramatically. I said I was cutting down on screens, which was true.

---

The harder part came afterwards, and from the other direction.

Once you stop saying what you hold, you start noticing how much other people say. The friend who announces every entry and never the exit. The voice online that has been bullish on the same thing for a year and is never counted against it. I found that I wanted to argue with them. I had the count on my side, after all. I knew what a forecast is worth when nobody keeps score.

That is the urge the chapter turns to next. *Blunt the sharp, untie the tangled, soften the glare.* An argument against someone else's position is a position too. It has an audience, and it has a price at which you look foolish. So I do not do that either. If someone asks me what I think of a stock, I tell them I do not know, which is nearly always true, and that I can tell them how I would test the idea, which is the only thing I am sure of.

When I have to talk, I talk about rules. A rule can be argued with in daylight. It does not lose face when it is wrong, because it was never anyone's face.

---

There is a line near the end of the chapter that I like better each year: *he cannot be honoured and cannot be shamed.* The friends in that chat could not have shamed me if they had wanted to. They could only do what they did, which was cheer, and the cheering was the danger.

A trader nobody is watching can leave at the stop without saving face. I did not understand how valuable that was until I had given it away for two days.
