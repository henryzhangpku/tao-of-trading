# 77. Bending the Bow

*Part II: The Virtue · 《天之道，其犹张弓与》*

## The Verse

天之道，其犹张弓与？高者抑之，下者举之；有余者损之，不足者补之。

天之道，损有余而补不足。人之道则不然，损不足以奉有余。

孰能有余以奉天下？唯有道者。是以圣人为而不恃，功成而不处，其不欲见贤。

The way of heaven: is it not like stringing a bow? What is high is pressed down, what is low is lifted. What has too much is reduced, what has too little is filled.

The way of heaven takes from what has too much and gives to what has too little. The way of man is not so. It takes from what has too little to serve what has too much.

Who can take what he has too much of and serve the world with it? Only one who has the Way. So the sage acts and does not lean on it. He finishes the work and does not stay in it. He has no wish to show his worth.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## What You Know

Money goes to money. That is how the world works, and the market is the purest version of it. Add to your winners and cut your losers. When a position is working, feed it; it has proved itself, and that is where your capital belongs. Take from the dead weight, the idle cash, the small names going nowhere, and put it behind what is moving. The great years are made by one or two trades held big and long. Concentrate. Press your advantage. When the getting is good, take more, because the getting will not always be good.

## The Other Way

1. *Is it not like stringing a bow?* A bow shoots because the tension is even. Pull one end too far and it does not shoot further. It cracks.

2. *What is high is pressed down.* The position that has grown to fill the account is not your best idea. It is your largest risk, dressed as your best idea.

3. *What is low is lifted.* Cash, rest, the quiet corner of the account. These are what you raid first, and these are what hold you up.

4. *The way of man takes from what has too little to serve what has too much.* Selling the small, dull positions to buy more of the hot one. Cutting your sleep to watch it overnight. It is the same move.

5. Fortune reverts. Not on a schedule and not because anything is owed. A long run of luck in one name is the thing most likely to be pressed down next.

6. Ask of the big winner: if I were starting today, would I buy this much? If not, you are not holding it. It is holding you.

7. A good month is excess too. Take some off the top and move it out of the account, where the market cannot reach it.

8. *Who can take what he has too much of and serve the world with it?* After a strong run, give the extra to what has gone short: the family, the body, the days off.

9. *He acts and does not lean on it.* The big win is not a reason to trust yourself more. Lean on it and it becomes the weight that bends you.

10. *He has no wish to show his worth.* The position you most want to show someone is usually the one to trim. Taking less is how you keep more.

## The Story

It started as a small position, a name you liked and bought at a third of your usual size. It went up. You added on the first pullback, as you were taught. It went up again. You added again. Within a couple of months it had doubled, and now it is the largest thing in the account by far.

Every day it works, it gets more of you. You sell two small positions that were doing nothing to buy more of it. The cash you usually keep aside shrinks, and then it is gone. You check the name before bed and again when you wake. You read everything written about it, and most of it agrees with you, which you take as a sign.

On the screen it is your best trade. In your head it has become the trade that proves you are a real trader. You have stopped thinking of it as a position. It is a part of you.

---

Then one morning it opens down eight percent on nothing you can find. It is not a disaster for the company. It is a disaster for you, because of how much of it you own. In one session the account gives back a month. It bounces a little. You hold, because selling now would be selling the low. It slides again the next week.

Look at how it happened. Nothing in the name changed much. What changed was its share of you. *The way of man takes from what has too little to serve what has too much.* You took from the cash, from the other positions, from your sleep, and fed it all to the one thing that already had the most. You did not see that you were stringing a bow with all the pull at one end.

This is how most of us were taught to treat success in any part of life. Double down on what works. Pour everything into the thing that is winning. Starve the rest; the rest can wait. It sounds like good sense. It is a bow pulled crooked.

---

The other way does not ask you to be clever about the top. Nobody knows the top. It asks you to keep the bow even.

Once a week, when the market is closed, you look at the account as if you had just been handed it. For each position you ask one question: would I buy this much today? The big winner comes up. The honest answer is no, not this much. So on Monday you sell enough to bring it back to the size you would choose fresh. It feels wrong. Everyone says you never trim a winner. The name goes up the next day, and you feel foolish for a week.

You take part of the month's gains and move it out of the account altogether. You put cash back in the corner where it belongs. You give back the sleep: the name is not checked before bed. What was high is pressed down a little. What was low is lifted a little.

When the eight-percent morning comes, as some morning does, it is a bad morning, not a bad month. You can look at it. You can even think about it, because there is room in the account and room in you.

---

The old Chinese view turns the old advice the other way round. You were told that more goes to whoever already has more, and that the winner should be fed. Heaven does the opposite, and the market, over time, behaves like heaven, not like man. What has run too far tends to be pressed down. What has been neglected tends to be lifted. The trader who trims his excess and fills his lack gives up part of every long run. In return, he is still there when the run ends.

## Try This

This weekend, go through your positions as if someone had just handed you the account. Next to each, write the size you would buy today, starting fresh. Where the real size is far above that number, bring it down on Monday, and move a small part of any good month out of the account. Watch what changes.
