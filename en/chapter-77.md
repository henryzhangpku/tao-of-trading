# 77. Bending the Bow

*Part II: The Virtue · 《天之道，其犹张弓与》*

## The Verse

天之道，其犹张弓与？高者抑之，下者举之；有余者损之，不足者补之。

天之道，损有余而补不足。人之道则不然，损不足以奉有余。

孰能有余以奉天下？唯有道者。是以圣人为而不恃，功成而不处，其不欲见贤。

The way of heaven: is it not like stringing a bow? What is high is pressed down, what is low is lifted. What has too much is reduced, what has too little is filled.

The way of heaven takes from what has too much and gives to what has too little. The way of man is not so. It takes from what has too little to serve what has too much.

Who can take what he has too much of and serve the world with it? Only one who has the Way. So the sage acts and does not lean on it. He finishes the work and does not stay in it. He has no wish to show his worth.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## The Trader

1. Your own results obey the bow. The high stretch is pressed down, the low stretch is lifted. Most of what looks like skill in a hot month and failure in a cold one is the string going back to rest.

2. So the best month of a test is the least trustworthy month in it. Take it out and look at what is left.

3. If nothing is left, there was never a method. There was a month.

4. Do the same with the best single trade, and the best single name. A record that stands only on its tallest tree is one storm from flat ground.

5. The way of man takes from what has too little to serve what has too much. After a hot run, the trader adds size to the thing that just ran, and starves the quiet rules that were doing the ordinary work.

6. Do not raise size because the last stretch was good. Raise it, if ever, because the long record is good and the size was always too small for it.

7. Do not cut a sound rule because the last stretch was cold. Cold stretches are what the string does on the way down.

8. Expect your next month to be closer to your average than your last month was. That is not pessimism. It is the bow.

9. The sage finishes the work and does not stay in it. Close the good month, write it down, and do not move in.

10. He has no wish to show his worth. A trader who needs the best month to be the real one will keep trading until it is proven, and it will not be.

## The Story

The idea came from the morning list. Each night a language model on my desk reads the day's results and writes down what it would try next, and one morning it suggested something I liked at once: buy the strongest names on the day after the whole market has had a violent fall, and hold them for a few weeks. The argument was a good one. In a panic everything is sold together, the good with the bad, and the good should be the first to recover.

The model proposes; the rules decide. The suggestion went into the ledger as a trial, with its definition written down before anything was run: what counts as a violent fall, what counts as strongest, how long to hold, what it costs. Then it went to the gate.

---

The first picture was lovely. Over the whole history the line rose, not smoothly but with conviction, and it kept rising after costs and after twice the costs. It survived the split into halves, though one half carried more than the other. I remember thinking that I would enjoy writing it up.

The next question on the gate is the plain one: take out the single best month and draw it again.

What was left was flat. Not falling. Flat, in the way a road is flat after you have driven over the only hill on it. Every bit of rise the first picture had shown me lived in one month.

I went and looked at the month. I did not need to look very hard, because I remembered it. It was one of the great rebounds, the few weeks after a fall that everybody alive at the time remembers, when the market turned and went straight up and anything you had bought on the worst day looked like genius by the end of it. The rule had been built to buy on violent falls. That fall was the most violent in the data, and the rebound after it was the largest. The rule had caught it, as any rule of that shape would have.

Everything else in the history, all the other violent falls and their rebounds, added up to a long walk on level ground, paying a toll at every gate.

---

I took out the best single name as well, the way the gate asks. That made no difference, because by then there was nothing left to take it out of. There had never been a method. There had been a month.

*The way of heaven: is it not like stringing a bow? What is high is pressed down, what is low is lifted.* The month was the high end of the bow. A test that leans on it is a test of whether that month will come again, and on schedule. It will not. Something like it will come, at some point, and nobody can tell you when, and a rule that is flat for years while it waits is a rule that will be switched off long before it is paid.

I marked the trial as failed in the ledger and gave the reason in one line. The count went up by one. That is the part people skip, and the part that matters most: the next idea on that desk has to clear a bar that knows about this one.

---

I would have left it there, except that the rebound sent me looking for something else.

The month the rule had leaned on was also, as it happened, close to the best month I ever had as a trader, years before I built any of this. I had bought into a fall, more or less by nerve, and the market had turned under me, and for a few weeks everything I touched went up. I remember what I concluded. I concluded that I was good at this.

I still had the notebooks from that time, so I read them. The month was there, and then the months after it. They were ordinary. Some good, some bad, most of them closer to nothing than to that month. Nobody reading the notebook would pick out the trader of that one month in the trader of the following year. The string had gone back to rest, as strings do.

And I found something worse in the notebook than ordinary months. In the weeks after the hot one, my size had gone up. Not by a rule. By mood. I had taken from the dull things that had been doing their quiet work and given to whatever had just run. *The way of man takes from what has too little to serve what has too much.* It was there in my own handwriting: the hot month believed, and the size raised to honour it, just as the bow was turning back.

---

Two things on my desk changed afterwards. Neither is clever.

The first is the order of the gate's report. It used to show the full line first and the line without its best month further down, where I could admire the one before I read the other. Now it is the other way round. The first picture on the page is what is left after the best month is taken out. If that picture is nothing, I see nothing first, and the beautiful line, when I get to it, looks like what it is: one month, wearing a history as a coat.

The second is that size on my own books is set from code and changed on a calendar, never in the weeks after a good stretch. If the long record ever says the size was too small, I will raise it on a quiet day, and the change will be written down with its date.

*The sage finishes the work and does not stay in it.* I think about that month sometimes, the real one, the one I traded by nerve. It was a good month. I closed it a long time ago. I just try not to live there.
