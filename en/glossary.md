# Glossary

*The words this book leans on, in the Chinese, with the pinyin, the plain meaning, where Laozi uses them, and what they mean at a trading desk.*

Chapter numbers refer to the received Wang Bi text. A word Laozi uses in many places is given its main chapters, not all of them. Where a term is not Laozi's at all, the entry says so and says whose it is.

The twelve central terms come first. The others are the words that the first chapters of this book use most, in the order they appear in the text.

---

## The twelve

### 无为 · wúwéi — not forcing

**Plain meaning.** Literally "without doing." Not idleness: acting without straining against the grain of things, so that nothing is done that did not need doing.

**In Laozi.** Chapters 2 (处无为之事, "keeps to the work of not forcing"), 3, 37 (道常无为而无不为, "the Tao does nothing, and nothing is left undone"), 43, 48, 57, 63, 64.

**At the desk.** The trade not forced. No entry because you are bored, behind, or have a free morning. Doing nothing is a position, and on most days it is the right one. In a system, wu wei is the rule that runs without being nudged: the model proposes, the rules decide, and nobody reaches in to help. It is not the absence of work. All the work went into the rule before the market opened.

### 知止 · zhī zhǐ — knowing when to stop

**Plain meaning.** "Know stopping": to know where the limit is, and to stop there.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 32 (知止可以不殆, "knowing when to stop, you can avoid danger") and chapter 44 (知足不辱，知止不殆, "know contentment and you are not disgraced; know when to stop and you are not in danger"). Its twin, 知足, knowing enough, is in chapters 33, 44 and 46.

**At the desk.** The whole of risk management in two characters. The daily loss limit that switches the screen off. The time stop. The target written before the entry. The strategy retired when it stops doing what it was tested to do. The point is that the stop is *known*, written down in advance, not discovered in the middle of a bad afternoon.

### 朴 · pǔ — the uncarved block

**Plain meaning.** Raw wood before the carver touches it. Plainness; the simple thing before it is shaped for a use.

**In Laozi.** Chapters 15, 19 (见素抱朴, "show the plain, hold the uncarved"), 28 (朴散则为器, "the block, cut up, becomes tools"), 32, 37, 57.

**At the desk.** The plain rule before it is decorated. Every filter added to a backtest carves the block a little: it fits the past better and, usually, the future worse. A three-line rule that works after costs is pu. A twenty-line rule that works only on the years it was fitted to is a carving of last year. When in doubt, take a filter away, not add one.

### 虚 · xū — emptiness

**Plain meaning.** Empty, hollow, open; the space that is not filled.

**In Laozi.** Chapters 3 (虚其心, "empty their hearts"), 5 (虚而不屈, the bellows, "empty yet never exhausted") and 16 (致虚极, "attain utmost emptiness"). The same idea, under the word 冲, is the empty vessel of chapter 4, and the hub of chapter 11 is useful because of what is not there.

**At the desk.** Cash is a position. Unused buying power is capacity, not waste: the room the next good trade will need and the room the next bad week will take. Xu is also the mind at the start of the day: flat, carrying no position from yesterday's argument, no score to settle.

### 势 · shì — the lie of the land; momentum of position

**Plain meaning.** Configuration, potential, the force a thing has because of where it stands. A boulder at the top of a slope has shi.

**In Laozi.** Only once, chapter 51: 道生之，德畜之，物形之，势成之, "the Tao gives birth, virtue rears, things give shape, circumstance completes." The word belongs more to the strategists: Sun Tzu gives it a chapter (*The Art of War*, ch. 5, 兵势). Mark Spitznagel builds much of *The Dao of Capital* on it; I credit it to him there and to Sun Tzu here.

**At the desk.** Position before force. The trade with shi is the one where the ground has already tilted, and you only need to be small and present. Shi is why you size by the waves (chapter 8) and enter where the market is deep (chapter 6): you arrange where you stand, then let the slope do the work.

### 迂 · yū — the roundabout way

**Plain meaning.** Winding, indirect, the long way round.

**In Laozi.** *Not in the Tao Te Ching.* The phrase 以迂为直, "make the roundabout route the direct one," is Sun Tzu (*The Art of War*, ch. 7, 军争). Spitznagel takes it as the *Umweg* of the Austrian economists, the detour through patient capital, and makes it the heart of his book. Laozi's nearest lines are 曲则全，枉则直, "bend and be whole, crooked and be straight" (chapter 22), 反者道之动 (chapter 40) and 将欲歙之，必固张之 (chapter 36).

**At the desk.** Giving up a little now to arrive sooner. The forward test that runs a full season before anything is changed. The hundred trades skipped to take ten. On a trading day, yu is the limit order resting where the price must come, instead of the market order chasing it. The long way round is the short way to the end of the year with the account intact.

### 反 · fǎn — return, reversal

**Plain meaning.** To turn back, to go the other way; also, the opposite.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 40 (反者道之动，弱者道之用, "returning is the motion of the Tao; weakness is its use"), chapter 25 (远曰反, "far away means returning"), chapter 65 (与物反矣) and chapter 78 (正言若反, "straight words seem to be their opposite").

**At the desk.** Two things, both arithmetic. First, extremes do not last: the whirlwind does not outlast the morning, the stretched move is often near its end, your best month is likely to be followed by a more ordinary one. Second, the reversal of the trader's instinct: the hurry that makes you slow, the size that makes you small. *Slow is fast* is this book's version of fan. It is my line, not Laozi's.

### 柔 · róu — soft, yielding

**Plain meaning.** Soft, supple, pliant; what bends without breaking.

**In Laozi.** Chapters 36 (柔弱胜刚强, "the soft and weak overcome the hard and strong"), 43 (天下之至柔，驰骋天下之至坚, "the softest thing rides over the hardest"), 52 (守柔曰强, "holding to the soft is called strength"), 76 (the living are soft, the dead are stiff) and 78 (nothing is softer than water).

**At the desk.** Small size, defined risk, the stop. These are the soft things, and they outlast every hard conviction. The trader who must be right is rigid and breaks once. The one who takes the small loss at the named price bends and is still trading next season. Soft is not weak. Water is soft, and it cuts stone.

### 不争 · bù zhēng — not contending

**Plain meaning.** Not fighting, not competing, not pushing against.

**In Laozi.** Chapters 3, 8 (夫唯不争，故无尤, "only because it does not contend is it beyond reproach"), 22 (夫唯不争，故天下莫能与之争, "because he does not contend, no one under heaven can contend with him"), 66, 68, 73 and 81, the book's last line: 为而不争, "act, and do not contend." Note: the line from chapter 22 is often credited to Sun Tzu. It is Laozi.

**At the desk.** Do not argue with the market's weather. You cannot lose an argument you never started. Not contending is the trend rule sitting flat through a choppy month, the trader who does not trade the conversation, and the one who stays out of the crowded move. It is also not competing with other traders: no leaderboards, no screenshots, no one to be right to.

### 自然 · zìrán — so of itself

**Plain meaning.** Not "nature" in the modern sense. "Self-so": what happens of its own accord, without being made to.

**In Laozi.** Chapters 17 (百姓皆谓我自然, "the people all say: we did it ourselves"), 23 (希言自然, "to speak little is natural"), 25 (道法自然, "the Tao follows what is so of itself"), 51 and 64 (以辅万物之自然, "he helps the ten thousand things be what they are").

**At the desk.** The market moves by itself; your job is to stand where its movement can reach you, not to push it. A good process is ziran: the day runs from the first log line to the last without anyone having to intervene, and afterwards there is nothing to explain. A rule that needs your help every day is not running by itself, and is not yet a rule.

### 啬 · sè — frugality, sparing

**Plain meaning.** Thrift; holding back; using little and keeping the rest.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 59 only: 治人事天，莫若啬, "in ruling people and serving heaven, nothing is better than frugality," and 夫唯啬，是谓早服, "only frugality means preparing early."

**At the desk.** Costs are the one enemy you can control. Commissions, spread, slippage, and the churn of too many trades. Test every idea at double the costs you expect before you believe it. Se is also frugal with attention and with risk: spend little of the account on any one idea, and keep the rest for the day you need it. Frugality is preparation done early.

### 慈 · cí — compassion, gentle care

**Plain meaning.** Kindness; the care of a parent for a child.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 67, the first of the three treasures: 我有三宝……一曰慈，二曰俭，三曰不敢为天下先, "I have three treasures: the first is compassion, the second is frugality, the third is not daring to be first under heaven." 慈故能勇, "from compassion comes courage." The word also appears in chapters 18 and 19.

**At the desk.** Patience with yourself. You will break your own rules; the response is a written lesson, not a punishment trade. Compassion is also what lets you be brave in the right way: the trader who does not need to prove anything can afford to take the stop, sit out the week, and wait for the next session after a loss.

## Other words the first chapters lean on

### 道 · dào — the way

**Plain meaning.** A road; a way of going; also, to say.

**In Laozi.** Everywhere; the book is named for it. Chapter 1 opens with the pun: 道可道，非常道, "the way that can be spoken is not the constant way." Chapter 25: 吾不知其名，字之曰道, "I do not know its name; I call it the Tao."

**At the desk.** Not a mystery. In this book the Tao is the arithmetic of survival and the discipline of leaving a rule alone. The way that can be spoken is your written rule; the constant way is the market, which no rule fully describes. Use the first; do not confuse it with the second.

### 德 · dé — virtue, power

**Plain meaning.** Not moral virtue so much as the power a thing has when it is true to its way: the potency of a well-made tool.

**In Laozi.** The second half of the book, 德经, starts with it: 上德不德，是以有德, "high virtue does not show itself as virtue, so it has virtue" (chapter 38). Also chapters 10, 21, 51, 55.

**At the desk.** Discipline you do not have to announce. The trader with de does not post resolutions; they keep one rule. A system with de does what it says it will do on the bad day, and nobody notices because nothing goes wrong.

### 名 · míng — name

**Plain meaning.** Name, label; also reputation.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 1 (名可名，非常名, "the name that can be named is not the constant name"), chapter 32 (始制有名……知止所以不殆, "once names appear, know when to stop"), chapter 44 (名与身孰亲, "your name or your life: which is dearer?").

**At the desk.** "Momentum," "reversion," "breakout," "regime": names given to prices after they moved. The prices did not know their names. Use names because you must, and hold them loosely. And once you have named a trade, know when to stop adding to it.

### 有无相生 · yǒu wú xiāng shēng — having and not having give birth to each other

**Plain meaning.** Opposites define each other: there is no long without short, no high without low.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 2, and the pairs that follow it (难易相成，长短相较). The same thought runs through chapter 11 (有之以为利，无之以为用, "what is there gives benefit; what is not there gives use") and chapter 40 (有生于无).

**At the desk.** A win rate means nothing without the payoff beside it. The breakeven win rate is the average loss divided by the average win plus the average loss: risk one to make three and you must be right one time in four. Change one end of the stick and the other moves. Nothing is free.

### 无欲 · wú yù — without desire

**Plain meaning.** Without wanting; looking without craving a result.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 1 (常无欲以观其妙，常有欲以观其徼, "without desire you see its subtlety; with desire you see only its edges"), chapter 3 (不见可欲, "do not display the desirable"), chapters 37, 57, 64 (欲不欲, "want what others do not want").

**At the desk.** Look at a backtest wanting something and you see only the outline of your own idea. That is why the test is written down before it runs and every try is counted. Wu yu is also the screen built not to display the desirable: show the size, the stop, the reason; make the running profit small and grey.

### 冲 · chōng — empty, hollow (of a vessel)

**Plain meaning.** Hollow, like an empty cup; in some uses, a blending of forces.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 4 (道冲，而用之或不盈, "the way is empty, and using it never fills it"), chapter 42 (冲气以为和) and chapter 45 (大盈若冲, "great fullness seems empty").

**At the desk.** The empty vessel. An account is useful because part of it is empty. Size so that a whole day of losses leaves the bowl mostly empty. A fully committed account cannot act on the one setup that matters.

### 刍狗 · chú gǒu — straw dogs

**Plain meaning.** Dogs made of straw for a sacrifice, honoured during the rite and thrown away after.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 5 only: 天地不仁，以万物为刍狗, "heaven and earth are not kind; they treat the ten thousand things as straw dogs."

**At the desk.** The market does not know you are there. Not cruelty: indifference. A perfect setup can fail for no reason you will ever find. Look in the logs once, and if nothing is there, leave it there. Do not invent a reason; a story fitted to one loss is the first filter of the next overfitted rule.

### 守中 · shǒu zhōng — hold to the centre

**Plain meaning.** Keep to the middle; stay with what is essential.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 5: 多言数穷，不如守中, "much talk soon runs dry; better to hold to the centre."

**At the desk.** After a loss, the explanations multiply. Most of them are noise. Hold to the centre: the size, the stop, the rule. The post-mortem that runs longer than the trade has stopped being useful.

### 谷 · gǔ — the valley

**Plain meaning.** A valley; low ground where water gathers.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 6 (谷神不死, "the valley spirit never dies"), chapters 15, 28 (为天下谷, "be the valley of the world"), 39, 41 (上德若谷, "high virtue is like a valley") and 66 (江海所以能为百谷王者, rivers and seas rule the hundred valleys by lying below them).

**At the desk.** Trade where the water still flows: deep markets, tight spreads, real volume. Refuse the entry when the market is thin; never block the exit in any market. Small, low and liquid: the trader who stays in the valley survives every season.

### 后其身 · hòu qí shēn — put yourself last

**Plain meaning.** Place your own interest behind; do not push to the front.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 7 (后其身而身先，外其身而身存, "he puts himself last and finds himself first; puts himself outside and finds himself kept"), and chapter 66.

**At the desk.** The account before the ego; survival before return. Write the rule as if someone else would run it, and then let someone else run it, even if that someone is a machine. The strategy that does not need to win today is the one still running next year.

### 水 · shuǐ — water

**Plain meaning.** Water.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 8 (上善若水, "the highest good is like water"), chapter 78 (天下莫柔弱于水，而攻坚强者莫之能胜, "nothing is softer than water, yet nothing beats it against the hard"). Also chapters 32 and 66, as rivers and seas.

**At the desk.** Take the shape of the market; do not argue with its weather. Size by the size of the waves: when the market swings wider, the same risk buys a smaller position. Move when the ground tilts, not when you are bored. Water is the book's central image of the soft that wins.

### 盈 · yíng — full, filled to the brim

**Plain meaning.** Full; overflowing.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 9 (持而盈之，不如其已, "to hold it and fill it to the brim is not as good as stopping in time"), chapters 4, 15 (保此道者不欲盈, "who keeps this way does not want to be full"), 22, 39, 45.

**At the desk.** Full size at the top is how gains are given back. A large open profit is a large open risk with a pleasant name. Take what the written target says to take; let the trade run on without you if it will.

### 功成身退 · gōng chéng shēn tuì — the work done, step back

**Plain meaning.** When the task is complete, withdraw; do not stay to claim it.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 9 (功遂身退，天之道, "when the work is done, step back: that is the way of heaven"). The same thought in chapter 2 (功成而弗居), 17, 34 and 77.

**At the desk.** The day's plan is finished; close the screen. Do not dwell in the good month. The result belongs to the rule, not to you, and the hour after a target is hit is when "one more" is offered.

### 难得之货 · nán dé zhī huò — goods hard to get

**Plain meaning.** Rare, prized things; what is coveted because it is scarce.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 3 (不贵难得之货，使民不为盗), chapter 12 and chapter 64.

**At the desk.** The perfect entry, the exact low, the last tick of the move. Chasing the rare price is how you miss the ordinary one. A rule that needs a hard-to-get fill to work does not work.

### 慎终如始 · shèn zhōng rú shǐ — careful at the end as at the beginning

**Plain meaning.** Take the same care finishing a thing as starting it.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 64: 民之从事，常于几成而败之。慎终如始，则无败事, "people at their work most often fail when it is nearly done; be as careful at the end as at the beginning, and nothing will fail."

**At the desk.** Do not touch the frozen rule in the last weeks of a forward test, however bad the week. Do not loosen the exit because the trade is nearly at target. The test that is changed at the end has to start again from the beginning.
