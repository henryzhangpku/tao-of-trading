# Glossary

*The words this book leans on, in the Chinese, with the pinyin, the plain meaning, where Laozi uses them, and what they mean for the trader.*

Chapter numbers refer to the received Wang Bi text. A word Laozi uses in many places is given its main chapters, not all of them. Where a term is not Laozi's at all, the entry says so and says whose it is.

The twelve central terms come first. The others are the words that the first chapters of this book use most, in the order they appear in the text.

---

## The twelve

### 无为 · wúwéi — not forcing

**Plain meaning.** Literally "without doing." Not idleness: acting without straining against the grain of things, so that nothing is done that did not need doing.

**In Laozi.** Chapters 2 (处无为之事, "keeps to the work of not forcing"), 3, 37 (道常无为而无不为, "the Tao does nothing, and nothing is left undone"), 43, 48, 57, 63, 64.

**For the trader.** You were taught that money is made by doing: find the trade, take it, manage it, find the next. The other way: the trade not forced. No entry because you are bored, behind, or have a free morning. Doing nothing is a position, and on most days it is the right one. Wu wei is not idleness. It is the hand kept off the order ticket until the market asks for it, not the urge. The trader who does less is not working less. He is wasting less.

### 知止 · zhī zhǐ — knowing when to stop

**Plain meaning.** "Know stopping": to know where the limit is, and to stop there.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 32 (知止所以不殆, "knowing when to stop is how you stay out of danger") and chapter 44 (知足不辱，知止不殆, "know contentment and you are not disgraced; know when to stop and you are not in danger"). Its twin, 知足, knowing enough, is in chapters 33, 44 and 46.

**For the trader.** You were taught that the great trades are the ones you ride all the way, and the great days are the ones you keep pressing. The other way: the whole of risk management in two characters. The daily loss limit that switches the screen off. The time stop. The target set before the entry. The day you walk away up and the day you walk away down, both at a line drawn in the morning. The point is that the stop is *known*, decided while you are calm, not discovered in the middle of a bad afternoon.

### 朴 · pǔ — the uncarved block

**Plain meaning.** Raw wood before the carver touches it. Plainness; the simple thing before it is shaped for a use.

**In Laozi.** Chapters 15, 19 (见素抱朴, "show the plain, hold the uncarved"), 28 (朴散则为器, "the block, cut up, becomes tools"), 32, 37, 57.

**For the trader.** You were taught that the better trader has the more sophisticated read: more conditions, more confirmations, a cleverer story. The other way: the plain setup before it is decorated. Every condition added after a loss carves the block a little: it explains the last trade better and the next one worse. A setup you can say in one breath is pu. One that needs a page of exceptions is a carving of the trade that hurt you. When in doubt, take something away, not add it.

### 虚 · xū — emptiness

**Plain meaning.** Empty, hollow, open; the space that is not filled.

**In Laozi.** Chapters 3 (虚其心, "empty their hearts"), 5 (虚而不屈, the bellows, "empty yet never exhausted") and 16 (致虚极, "attain utmost emptiness"). The same idea, under the word 冲, is the empty vessel of chapter 4, and the hub of chapter 11 is useful because of what is not there.

**For the trader.** You were taught that cash is lazy money and the account should always be working. The other way: cash is a position. Unused buying power is capacity, not waste: the room the next good trade will need and the room the next bad week will take. Xu is also the mind at the start of the day: flat, carrying no position from yesterday's argument, no score to settle.

### 势 · shì — the lie of the land; momentum of position

**Plain meaning.** Configuration, potential, the force a thing has because of where it stands. A boulder at the top of a slope has shi.

**In Laozi.** Only once, chapter 51: 道生之，德畜之，物形之，势成之, "the Tao gives birth, virtue rears, things give shape, circumstance completes." The word belongs more to the strategists: Sun Tzu gives it a chapter (*The Art of War*, ch. 5, 兵势). Mark Spitznagel builds much of *The Dao of Capital* on it; I credit it to him in the afterword and to Sun Tzu here.

**For the trader.** You were taught that effort and conviction make a trade work. The other way: position before force. The trade with shi is the one where the ground has already tilted, and you only need to be small and present. Shi is why you size by the waves (chapter 8) and enter where the market is deep (chapter 6): you arrange where you stand, then let the slope do the work. A trade you have to push uphill is effort spent against the ground.

### 迂 · yū — the roundabout way

**Plain meaning.** Winding, indirect, the long way round.

**In Laozi.** *Not in the Tao Te Ching.* The phrase 以迂为直, "make the roundabout route the direct one," is Sun Tzu (*The Art of War*, ch. 7, 军争). Spitznagel takes it as the *Umweg* of the Austrian economists, the detour through patient capital, and makes it the heart of his book. Laozi's nearest lines are 曲则全，枉则直, "bend and be whole, crooked and be straight" (chapter 22), 反者道之动 (chapter 40) and 将欲歙之，必固张之 (chapter 36).

**For the trader.** You were taught to take the straight line: see it, hit it, get there first. The other way: giving up a little now to arrive sooner. The hundred setups let pass to take ten. The week sat flat while the market sorts itself out. On a trading day, yu is the limit order resting where the price must come, instead of the market order chasing it. The long way round is the short way to the end of the year with the account intact.

### 反 · fǎn — return, reversal

**Plain meaning.** To turn back, to go the other way; also, the opposite.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 40 (反者道之动，弱者道之用, "returning is the motion of the Tao; weakness is its use"), chapter 25 (远曰反, "far away means returning"), chapter 65 (与物反矣) and chapter 78 (正言若反, "straight words seem to be their opposite").

**For the trader.** You were taught that the trend is your friend and a strong move keeps going. The other way, in two parts. First, extremes do not last: the whirlwind does not outlast the morning, the stretched move is often near its end, your best month is likely to be followed by a more ordinary one. Second, the reversal of the trader's instinct itself: the hurry that makes you slow, the size that makes you small, the urge to win it back that makes you lose more. *Slow is fast* is this book's version of fan. It is my line, not Laozi's.

### 柔 · róu — soft, yielding

**Plain meaning.** Soft, supple, pliant; what bends without breaking.

**In Laozi.** Chapters 36 (柔弱胜刚强, "the soft and weak overcome the hard and strong"), 43 (天下之至柔，驰骋天下之至坚, "the softest thing rides over the hardest"), 52 (守柔曰强, "holding to the soft is called strength"), 76 (the living are soft, the dead are stiff) and 78 (nothing is softer than water).

**For the trader.** You were taught that hard conviction wins and the tough trader survives. The other way: small size, defined risk, the stop. These are the soft things, and they outlast every hard conviction. The trader who must be right is rigid and breaks once. The one who takes the small loss at the named price bends and is still trading next season. Soft is not weak. Water is soft, and it cuts stone.

### 不争 · bù zhēng — not contending

**Plain meaning.** Not fighting, not competing, not pushing against.

**In Laozi.** Chapters 3, 8 (夫唯不争，故无尤, "only because it does not contend is it beyond reproach"), 22 (夫唯不争，故天下莫能与之争, "because he does not contend, no one under heaven can contend with him"), 66, 68, 73 and 81, the book's last line: 为而不争, "act, and do not contend." Note: the line from chapter 22 is often credited to Sun Tzu. It is Laozi.

**For the trader.** You were taught to fight for every tick and beat the other side. The other way: do not argue with the market's weather. You cannot lose an argument you never started. Not contending is sitting flat through a choppy week instead of trading against it, taking the loss without a fight, staying out of the crowded move. It is also not competing with other traders: no leaderboards, no screenshots, no one to be right to.

### 自然 · zìrán — so of itself

**Plain meaning.** Not "nature" in the modern sense. "Self-so": what happens of its own accord, without being made to.

**In Laozi.** Chapters 17 (百姓皆谓我自然, "the people all say: we did it ourselves"), 23 (希言自然, "to speak little is natural"), 25 (道法自然, "the Tao follows what is so of itself"), 51 and 64 (以辅万物之自然, "he helps the ten thousand things be what they are").

**For the trader.** You were taught that you make a trade work by managing it hard. The other way: the market moves by itself; your job is to stand where its movement can reach you, not to push it. A good day is ziran: the plan made in the morning runs to the close without being rescued, and afterwards there is nothing to explain. A trade that needs your help every ten minutes is not going your way. Let it go, or let it be.

### 啬 · sè — frugality, sparing

**Plain meaning.** Thrift; holding back; using little and keeping the rest.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 59 only: 治人事天，莫若啬, "in ruling people and serving heaven, nothing is better than frugality," and 夫唯啬，是谓早服, "only frugality means preparing early."

**For the trader.** You were taught that costs are small and the next trade might be the one. The other way: costs are the one enemy you can control. Commissions, spread, slippage, and the churn of too many trades: each small, together large. Se is also frugal with attention and with risk: spend little of the account on any one idea, little of yourself on any one morning, and keep the rest for the day you need it. Frugality is preparation done early.

### 慈 · cí — compassion, gentle care

**Plain meaning.** Kindness; the care of a parent for a child.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 67, the first of the three treasures: 我有三宝……一曰慈，二曰俭，三曰不敢为天下先, "I have three treasures: the first is compassion, the second is frugality, the third is not daring to be first under heaven." 慈故能勇, "from compassion comes courage." The word also appears in chapters 18 and 19.

**For the trader.** You were taught that discipline means being hard on yourself. The other way: patience with yourself. You will break your own rules; the answer is a quiet note and a smaller next trade, not a punishment trade. Compassion is also what lets you be brave in the right way: the trader who does not need to prove anything can afford to take the stop, sit out the week, and wait for the next session after a loss.

## Other words the first chapters lean on

### 道 · dào — the way

**Plain meaning.** A road; a way of going; also, to say.

**In Laozi.** Everywhere; the book is named for it. Chapter 1 opens with the pun: 道可道，非常道, "the way that can be spoken is not the constant way." Chapter 25: 吾不知其名，字之曰道, "I do not know its name; I call it the Tao."

**For the trader.** You were taught that the market is a code, and the good trader cracks it. The other way: not a code and not a mystery. In this book the Tao is the plain arithmetic of staying in the game and the steadiness to leave a good plan alone. The way that can be spoken is your plan, said in a sentence; the constant way is the market, which no plan fully describes. Use the first; do not confuse it with the second.

### 德 · dé — virtue, power

**Plain meaning.** Not moral virtue so much as the power a thing has when it is true to its way: the potency of a well-made tool.

**In Laozi.** The second half of the book, 德经, starts with it: 上德不德，是以有德, "high virtue does not show itself as virtue, so it has virtue" (chapter 38). Also chapters 10, 21, 51, 55.

**For the trader.** You were taught that discipline is willpower you summon on the hard day. The other way: discipline you do not have to announce. The trader with de does not post resolutions; he keeps one habit. He does what he said he would do on the bad day, and nobody notices, because nothing goes wrong. When it takes a great effort of will, the habit has already slipped.

### 名 · míng — name

**Plain meaning.** Name, label; also reputation.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 1 (名可名，非常名, "the name that can be named is not the constant name"), chapter 32 (始制有名……知止所以不殆, "once names appear, know when to stop"), chapter 44 (名与身孰亲, "your name or your life: which is dearer?").

**For the trader.** You were taught that naming the setup is half of trading it. The other way: "momentum," "reversal," "breakout," "short squeeze": names given to prices after they moved. The prices did not know their names. Use names because you must, and hold them loosely. And once you have named a trade, know when to stop adding to it.

### 有无相生 · yǒu wú xiāng shēng — having and not having give birth to each other

**Plain meaning.** Opposites define each other: there is no long without short, no high without low.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 2, and the pairs that follow it (难易相成，长短相较). The same thought runs through chapter 11 (有之以为利，无之以为用, "what is there gives benefit; what is not there gives use") and chapter 40 (有生于无).

**For the trader.** You were taught to want a high win rate, because winning often feels like trading well. The other way: a win rate means nothing without the payoff beside it. Risk one to make three and you break even being right one time in four; risk three to make one and you need three in four. Change one end of the stick and the other moves. The trader who must win often has quietly agreed to win small. Nothing is free.

### 无欲 · wú yù — without desire

**Plain meaning.** Without wanting; looking without craving a result.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 1 (常无欲以观其妙，常有欲以观其徼, "without desire you see its subtlety; with desire you see only its edges"), chapter 3 (不见可欲, "do not display the desirable"), chapters 37, 57, 64 (欲不欲, "want what others do not want").

**For the trader.** You were taught to want it: hungry traders make money. The other way: look at a chart wanting something and you see only the outline of your own hope. If you need to be long, every candle is a reason. Look before you have a position, a view or a target, while there is nothing yet to want. Wu yu is also not watching the open profit tick: check the size, the stop and the reason, and let the number stay small and grey.

### 冲 · chōng — empty, hollow (of a vessel)

**Plain meaning.** Hollow, like an empty cup; in some uses, a blending of forces.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 4 (道冲，而用之或不盈, "the way is empty, and using it never fills it"), chapter 42 (冲气以为和) and chapter 45 (大盈若冲, "great fullness seems empty").

**For the trader.** You were taught to put all your capital to work. The other way: the empty vessel. An account is useful because part of it is empty. Size so that a whole day of losses leaves the bowl mostly empty. A fully committed account cannot act on the one setup that matters.

### 刍狗 · chú gǒu — straw dogs

**Plain meaning.** Dogs made of straw for a sacrifice, honoured during the rite and thrown away after.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 5 only: 天地不仁，以万物为刍狗, "heaven and earth are not kind; they treat the ten thousand things as straw dogs."

**For the trader.** You were taught that the market rewards the trader who did the work and was right. The other way: the market does not know you are there. Not cruelty: indifference. A perfect setup can fail for no reason you will ever find. Look once, and if nothing is there, leave it there. Do not invent a reason; a story fitted to one loss becomes the habit that costs you the next trade. Take nothing personally.

### 守中 · shǒu zhōng — hold to the centre

**Plain meaning.** Keep to the middle; stay with what is essential.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 5: 多言数穷，不如守中, "much talk soon runs dry; better to hold to the centre."

**For the trader.** You were taught to study every loss until you understand it. The other way: after a loss, the explanations multiply, and most of them are noise. Hold to the centre: the size, the stop, the plan. The replay that runs longer than the trade has stopped being useful.

### 谷 · gǔ — the valley

**Plain meaning.** A valley; low ground where water gathers.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 6 (谷神不死, "the valley spirit never dies"), chapters 15, 28 (为天下谷, "be the valley of the world"), 39, 41 (上德若谷, "high virtue is like a valley") and 66 (江海所以能为百谷王者, rivers and seas rule the hundred valleys by lying below them).

**For the trader.** You were taught to go out and hunt for trades wherever they are. The other way: stay low, where the water gathers. Trade where it still flows: deep markets, tight spreads, real volume. Refuse the entry when the market is thin; never stand somewhere you cannot get out. Small, low and liquid: the trader who stays in the valley survives every season.

### 后其身 · hòu qí shēn — put yourself last

**Plain meaning.** Place your own interest behind; do not push to the front.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 7 (后其身而身先，外其身而身存, "he puts himself last and finds himself first; puts himself outside and finds himself kept"), and chapter 66.

**For the trader.** You were taught to put your P&L first. The other way: the account before the ego; survival before return. The trader who needs today's win to feel whole trades for himself and pays for it. The one who would take the same trade with someone else's money, and does not need to win today, is the one still trading next year.

### 水 · shuǐ — water

**Plain meaning.** Water.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 8 (上善若水, "the highest good is like water"), chapter 78 (天下莫柔弱于水，而攻坚强者莫之能胜, "nothing is softer than water, yet nothing beats it against the hard"). Also chapters 32 and 66, as rivers and seas.

**For the trader.** You were taught to be firm and stick to your guns. The other way: take the shape of the market; do not argue with its weather. Size by the size of the waves: when the market swings wider, the same risk buys a smaller position. Move when the ground tilts, not when you are bored. Water is the book's central image of the soft that wins.

### 盈 · yíng — full, filled to the brim

**Plain meaning.** Full; overflowing.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 9 (持而盈之，不如其已, "to hold it and fill it to the brim is not as good as stopping in time"), chapters 4, 15 (保此道者不欲盈, "who keeps this way does not want to be full"), 22, 39, 45.

**For the trader.** You were taught to let winners run and never leave money on the table. The other way: full size at the top is how gains are given back. A large open profit is a large open risk with a pleasant name. Take what you said you would take; if the move runs on, let it run on without you. The money left on the table was never in your account.

### 功成身退 · gōng chéng shēn tuì — the work done, step back

**Plain meaning.** When the task is complete, withdraw; do not stay to claim it.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 9 (功遂身退，天之道, "when the work is done, step back: that is the way of heaven"). The same thought in chapter 2 (功成而弗居), 17, 34 and 77.

**For the trader.** You were taught to press when you are hot. The other way: the day's plan is finished; close the screen. Do not dwell in the good month. The result belongs to the patience that made it, not to you, and the hour after a target is hit is exactly when "one more" is offered.

### 难得之货 · nán dé zhī huò — goods hard to get

**Plain meaning.** Rare, prized things; what is coveted because it is scarce.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 3 (不贵难得之货，使民不为盗), chapter 12 and chapter 64.

**For the trader.** You were taught that the best traders buy the exact low and sell the exact high. The other way: the perfect entry, the exact low, the last tick of the move are goods hard to get. Chasing the rare price is how you miss the ordinary one. A trade that only works with a perfect fill does not work.

### 慎终如始 · shèn zhōng rú shǐ — careful at the end as at the beginning

**Plain meaning.** Take the same care finishing a thing as starting it.

**In Laozi.** Chapter 64: 民之从事，常于几成而败之。慎终如始，则无败事, "people at their work most often fail when it is nearly done; be as careful at the end as at the beginning, and nothing will fail."

**For the trader.** You were taught that the hard part is getting in. The other way: trades are spoiled near the end. Do not loosen the exit because the trade is nearly at target. Do not let the last hour of a good day undo the first five, or a careless Friday undo a calm week. The care you gave the entry is owed again at the exit.
