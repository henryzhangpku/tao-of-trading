# 63. Act Without Acting

*Part II: The Virtue · 《为无为》*

## The Verse

为无为，事无事，味无味。大小多少，报怨以德。

图难于其易，为大于其细；天下难事，必作于易；天下大事，必作于细。是以圣人终不为大，故能成其大。

夫轻诺必寡信，多易必多难。是以圣人犹难之，故终无难矣。

Act without acting. Work without working. Taste what has no taste. Treat the small as great and the few as many. Answer resentment with virtue.

Plan the hard while it is easy. Do the great while it is small. The hard things of the world must be done while they are easy; the great things of the world must be done while they are small. So the sage never does great things, and that is how he accomplishes the great.

A light promise is seldom kept. Whoever finds much easy will meet much that is hard. So even the sage treats things as hard, and in the end he has no hardship.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## What You Know

Small things take care of themselves; it is the big problems that need your attention. Everyone learns this at work: do not sweat the small stuff, save yourself for what matters. A loss of a few cents is not worth acting on. You keep your effort for the big trade, the bad day, the drawdown that needs a real fix. And you know yourself. When you say you will stop after the next loss, you will. When you say you will review your trades this weekend, you will. A serious trader meets serious problems seriously, and does not waste energy fussing over small ones.

## The Other Way

1. *Plan the hard while it is easy.* A loss is easiest to take when it is small. Every tick makes it harder.

2. *Do the great while it is small.* The large drawdown was once a single trade a little past its stop.

3. *The hard things of the world must be done while they are easy.* Cutting a big loss takes courage. Cutting a small one takes a click.

4. *Treat the small as great.* Give the first few cents of loss the respect you would give a bad day. It is the same thing, early.

5. *The sage never does great things.* He never has to make the heroic exit, because he made the dull one in time.

6. *Taste what has no taste.* The small exit gives no relief and no story. That is how you know it was in time.

7. *A light promise is seldom kept.* "I will stop after one more" is a light promise. You have made it before.

8. *Whoever finds much easy will meet much that is hard.* The trader who expects discipline to come easily later meets the hard version of it.

9. *Even the sage treats things as hard.* Treat the small exit as hard, prepare for it as if it mattered, and it stays small.

10. *Answer resentment with virtue.* When the market takes a little from you, answer by taking the loss cleanly, not by demanding it back.

## The Story

The trade goes a little against you. Not much. It slips through the price where you said you would be wrong, by a few cents.

A few cents is nothing. You are not going to sell over a few cents and watch it bounce straight back. You give it to the next level, the round number just below. That is where the real support is, you tell yourself. If it breaks that, you are out.

It breaks that. Now the loss is not a few cents. It is a real number, the kind you feel in your stomach, and selling now would make it permanent. Selling a small loss was nothing. Selling this one is a decision, and you do not want to make a decision in a hurry. You will give it to the low of the day. If it breaks the low of the day, you are out. That is a promise.

It breaks the low of the day.

---

By the afternoon you have a big problem. The loss is three times what you planned, the kind of number that changes how the week goes. Now you need to do something serious. You sit and stare at it. You consider averaging down. You consider holding overnight. You consider everything except the thing you would have done without thinking two hours ago, when it cost a few cents.

When you finally sell, it is near the low, and you feel something like relief. That relief is what you were trying to buy all afternoon, and you paid for it with every tick.

In the evening you look for the big mistake. There is none. There was no moment when you did something reckless. There were five small moments, each easy to let go by, and three promises to yourself, each light enough to break.

---

*The hard things of the world must be done while they are easy.* This is not a rule about stops. Every trader knows to use one. It is a rule about when things are easy, and the answer is: early, before they look as if they matter. The small is where the great is decided.

So the other way is dull.

The price slips through your level by a few cents. You are out. You do not ask whether a few cents is enough to matter. You decided where you were wrong before you entered, and now you are there. The loss is small and gives you nothing, not even the satisfaction of a hard decision well made. *Taste what has no taste.*

Sometimes the stock bounces straight back. You watch it go up without you and feel foolish. That feeling is the whole cost of this way, and it is small. The trader who feels foolish a few times a month is in a very different place, after a year, from the one who has an afternoon like the first one every few months.

---

The promises are the same problem in another form. A light promise is a promise about later: *I'll get out if it breaks the low. I'll stop after one more. I'll review my trades this weekend.* Each sounds firm and each is easy to make, which is why each is easy to break. The trader who makes it is not the one who will have to keep it.

Most of what we are taught to admire is the great effort: the all-nighter, the rescue, the comeback. Laozi admires the person who never needed one. The sage treats even easy things as hard, he says, and so in the end he has nothing hard. He does not mean the sage worries. He means the sage does the small thing on time, because he knows what the small thing becomes.

## Try This

For the next week, make no promises to yourself about later in the session. Put the stop in the book at the moment you enter, so the order keeps the promise instead of you. If you decide to stop for the day, stop now rather than after the next trade, and write the day's review that evening rather than at the weekend.
