# 16. Return to the Root

*Part I: The Way · 《致虚极，守静笃》*

## The Verse

致虚极，守静笃。万物并作，吾以观复。

夫物芸芸，各复归其根。归根曰静，是谓复命。复命曰常，知常曰明。不知常，妄作，凶。

知常容，容乃公，公乃王，王乃天，天乃道，道乃久，没身不殆。

Reach the utmost emptiness. Hold stillness firmly. The ten thousand things rise together, and I watch them return.

Things flourish in their multitudes, and each goes back to its root. Going back to the root is called stillness. Stillness is called returning to one's nature. Returning to one's nature is called the constant. Knowing the constant is called clarity. Not knowing the constant, people act wildly, and come to harm.

Knowing the constant, you can hold everything. Holding everything, you are fair. Being fair, you are kingly. Being kingly, you are like heaven. Being like heaven, you are the way. Being the way, you last. To the end of your days you are not in danger.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## What You Know

Rest is something you earn. Work first, rest after: homework before television, the week before the weekend, a career before retirement. People who stop halfway are not serious. A trader at the open is on duty, and you were raised to respect duty. So you stay locked in from the opening bell to the close: eyes on the screen, hand near the mouse, lunch at the desk. Every minute away is a minute in which something could happen without you. You will rest when the session is over, or on Friday, or when the year has been good enough to take a proper holiday.

## The Other Way

1. *Reach the utmost emptiness. Hold stillness firmly.* The verse puts stillness first. It is where you start from, not a prize you collect once the work is done.

2. *Going back to the root is called stillness.* The root is not the weekend. It is a place you go back to at the same hours every day, before the work starts and in the middle of it.

3. *The ten thousand things rise together.* Every chart moves at once. The longer you stare at all of it without a break, the more of it seems to be calling your name.

4. *And I watch them return.* Watching needs somewhere to watch from. You cannot see the tide come back while you are swimming in it.

5. Attention held without a break from the open to the close is not focus. It is a battery running down that you have agreed to call focus.

6. Tired traders do not feel tired. They feel sure, quick and a little irritable, and they trade.

7. *Knowing the constant is called clarity.* Your constants are the few things that should not change during the day: your size, your stop, your limit for the day, the reason you trade at all. You can only see them clearly from the root.

8. *Not knowing the constant, people act wildly, and come to harm.* Wild action rarely looks wild. It is the order in the late afternoon that you would never have placed in the first hour.

9. *Each goes back to its root.* So should the day. Return at hours you fixed in advance, not when a loss or a lull finally forces you.

10. *To the end of your days you are not in danger.* That is not won in one great session. It is kept by returning, over and over, through ordinary ones.

## The Story

It is Monday and you are at the desk at twenty-five past nine with coffee. You will not leave it until four. You told yourself that on Sunday night, the way you tell yourself to finish a job properly. By eleven you have taken two trades, a decent win and a small loss, and you went straight from the second into looking for a third. Your eyes are dry. You notice it and blink and keep looking.

Lunch is a packet of crackers at the keyboard. The market goes quiet in the early afternoon, and quiet is uncomfortable after a morning like that, so you open more charts. A second watchlist. A news feed you do not usually read. Nothing is happening, and you are working hard at it.

---

At twenty to three something finally moves. A name you were not following jumps on volume. You buy it, and at a size a little larger than usual, because you have been here all day and it feels as if the day owes you something. You did not check it against anything. There was no time, and you were sure.

At ten past three it turns. You hold through your stop for a few minutes, then sell. You stay to the close anyway, because leaving early would make the whole day feel unfinished. After the close you sit replaying the afternoon, and you cannot say why you took that trade. The morning trader would not have taken it. The morning trader had gone home hours ago; only his chair was still occupied.

On the way to bed you think: I need a holiday. You will take one when the year has earned it.

---

Now the other Monday. The same open, the same coffee.

This time you sit down at nine with the screens still dark. For five minutes you do nothing. You feel your feet on the floor and your breath going in and out. Thoughts about the open arrive, a level, a headline, and you let them pass without following them. Then you read a small card taped under the screen. It says your size, your stop rule, your limit for the day. You know them by heart. You read them anyway. At five past nine you switch the screens on. Nothing in the market has changed. Something in you has.

The morning brings two trades, a win and a small loss, both at the size on the card. At noon you stand up, whatever is on the screen. You chose that time on Sunday; the market did not choose it for you. You eat somewhere without a screen and leave your phone in your pocket. When you come back at a quarter to one, before you touch the mouse, you read the card again. The early afternoon is just as quiet, and you let it be quiet. You do not open anything new.

At twenty to three the same name jumps. You see it. You also see, plainly, that it is nothing you trade and nothing you prepared for. It is a thing rising, and things that rise go back. You let it go. If it had been your setup, you would have taken it at your normal size, the size on the card. It was not.

At four you close the screen. There is nothing to replay. You are not exhausted, which feels strange, almost like you skipped something.

---

*Reach the utmost emptiness. Hold stillness firmly.* We are taught that rest is a reward: you earn it by grinding to the end, and then you get it, later. The old book sees it the other way round. Stillness is the root, and everything that happens in the day grows out of it and goes back into it. You return to it at fixed hours, at the start of the work and in the middle of it, not after.

Five minutes with the screens dark is not time off. It is part of the job.

## Try This

For the next five sessions, sit for five minutes before the open with the screens off, then read your constants (size, stop, daily limit) from a card before you switch them on. Before the week starts, pick a fixed time in the middle of the day, and at that time leave the desk for half an hour whatever the market is doing. Read the card again before you sit back down. At the end of the week, put your afternoon trades next to your morning ones and look at whether they were made by the same trader.
