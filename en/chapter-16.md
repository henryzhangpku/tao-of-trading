# 16. Return to the Root

*Part I: The Way · 《致虚极，守静笃》*

## The Verse

致虚极，守静笃。万物并作，吾以观复。

夫物芸芸，各复归其根。归根曰静，是谓复命。复命曰常，知常曰明。不知常，妄作，凶。

知常容，容乃公，公乃王，王乃天，天乃道，道乃久，没身不殆。

Reach the utmost emptiness. Hold stillness firmly. The ten thousand things rise together, and I watch them return.

Things flourish in their multitudes, and each goes back to its root. Going back to the root is called stillness. Stillness is called returning to one's nature. Returning to one's nature is called the constant. Knowing the constant is called clarity. Not knowing the constant, people act wildly, and come to harm.

Knowing the constant, you can hold everything. Holding everything, you are fair. Being fair, you are kingly. Being kingly, you are like heaven. Being like heaven, you are the way. Being the way, you last. To the end of your days you are not in danger.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## The Trader

1. Every day starts flat in the mind, whatever the account says. Yesterday's trade is closed. So is yesterday's mood.

2. Reach the utmost emptiness before the open, not after. The first hour before the screen is for emptying, not for filling.

3. The ten thousand things rise together: every name, every headline, every chart moving at once. I watch them return. Most of what rises at the open is back where it started by the close.

4. Each goes back to its root. Prices return, moods return, the urge to trade returns every morning. Knowing that it returns is what lets you not obey it.

5. The root of a trading day is the routine: the same checks, in the same order, every day, before anything is decided. The routine is the stillness. It does not care what happened yesterday.

6. Knowing the constant is clarity. The constant is not the market. It is your size, your stop, your loss limit. They are the same on a good morning and a bad one.

7. Not knowing the constant, people act wildly. Wild action is not dramatic. It is a size changed because of a feeling, a stop moved because of a hope.

8. To the end of your days you are not in danger. That is the whole aim. Not the best year: the account that is still open in the last one.

## The Story

The machine that runs the night's work finishes a little before five. I am usually awake before it is. For years that was a problem, and the story of how it stopped being one is mostly a story about a glass of water.

---

The old way was simple. I woke, reached for the laptop on the floor beside the bed, and opened the board. If the runs had finished, there were results: what had passed the gate overnight, what had failed, what the forward books had done, what the model had written about all of it. If they had not finished, I waited in the dark, refreshing.

Either way, by ten past five the night's results had decided my mood. A good night, and I went downstairs quick and pleased, already thinking about the next idea. A bad one, or a run that had broken, and I went down with a knot under the ribs and a list of things to fix. I would sit at the desk with the coffee and start fixing before I had read the morning's checks, and the morning's checks are the reason the desk exists.

One morning a run had failed. A data feed had sent half a file, and the job that used it had stopped. Nothing was wrong with any rule. Something was wrong with a pipe. I was at the desk by a quarter past five, and by six I had rewritten a piece of the loader, rerun the job, and was halfway into adjusting a threshold in a screen the failure had made me look at, before I noticed what I was doing. That screen was frozen. It was in a forward test, pinned by its fingerprint. I had been about to change it in the dark, in a hurry, because a pipe had broken.

I did not change it. But I sat there for a while with my hands off the keyboard, understanding that I had let the night reach into the morning and move them.

---

What I built after that is not clever. It is a sequence, and its only virtue is that it is the same every day.

I do not open the laptop in bed. It lives downstairs now. I get up, drink a glass of water standing in the kitchen, and look at nothing with a screen until the glass is empty. That is the whole of the first step. It takes a minute. It is the minute in which nothing has happened yet.

Then, at the desk, before the board, a card. It has the constants on it, written by hand: the risk per trade, the daily loss limit, the rule that nothing frozen is touched, the rule that no new rule is adopted on the day it is written. I read it. Then I open the file each number lives in and check that the file says what the card says. Most mornings this is pointless, because the numbers have not changed. That is why I do it. The constants are the part of the day that does not depend on the night.

Then yesterday. I open the notebook to yesterday's page, read the last line, and draw a line under it. Yesterday is closed. Whatever it was, its trades are in the record and its mood is not invited.

Only then the board.

---

The board says whatever it says. Some nights the gate passed something; most nights it did not. Some nights a pipe broke. When one does, I write it down and fix it after the checks, in daylight, as engineering, and not as a way of making the knot go away. The night's results are things to read, not things to obey.

*The ten thousand things rise together, and I watch them return.* That is how the board looks at five in the morning: everything moving at once, every number asking to be acted on. And most of it returns. A good night does not make the next night good. The broken pipe is fixed by eight. The urge to act on the board returns too, every single morning, as reliably as the machine finishes. I used to think the routine was meant to make the urge go away. It is not. It is what the urge returns to.

*Going back to the root is called stillness.* For most of my life I took that for a mystical line. The root of my trading day is now a glass of water, a hand-written card, the files checked against it, and a ruled line under yesterday. None of it is mystical. It is the same few things in the same order, and it has not once cared what happened overnight.

The machine still finishes before five, and I am still usually awake. Some mornings I lie there for a minute, knowing the results are waiting and not yet knowing what they are. I have come to like that minute. It is the emptiest part of the day, and the only part that is the same whether the night was good or bad.
