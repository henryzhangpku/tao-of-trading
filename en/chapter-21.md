# 21. Within the Dim

*Part I: The Way · 《孔德之容》*

## The Verse

孔德之容，惟道是从。道之为物，惟恍惟惚。

惚兮恍兮，其中有象；恍兮惚兮，其中有物。窈兮冥兮，其中有精；其精甚真，其中有信。

自古及今，其名不去，以阅众甫。吾何以知众甫之状哉？以此。

The bearing of great virtue follows the way alone. The way, as a thing, is elusive and indistinct.

Indistinct and elusive, yet within it there is an image. Elusive and indistinct, yet within it there is a thing. Deep and dark, yet within it there is an essence. The essence is very real, and within it there is proof.

From ancient times until now its name has not gone, and by it we see the beginning of all things. How do I know what the beginning of all things is like? By this.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## The Trader

1. Edge is dim. It does not show in any one trade. A single trade is mostly noise with a little edge stirred in.

2. Within the dim there is an image. Only in the count, over many trades, does the shape come out of the fog.

3. If you can see the edge plainly in one trade, it is not edge. It is a story about one trade.

4. Count every trial. The failed ones belong in the count. A ledger that keeps only the winners is a brochure.

5. Try one idea twenty ways and one of the twenty will look good by luck alone. The ledger knows it was twenty. Your memory will say one.

6. So the bar rises with every try. An idea tried many ways must clear a higher line than an idea tried once.

7. Deep and dark, yet within it there is an essence. You will not see a real edge clearly for a long time. That is not a fault in the edge. It is what edge is like.

8. The essence is very real, and within it there is proof. Proof is two numbers: how many times you looked, and what you found.

9. Its name has not gone. A real edge shows up in the first half of the history and in the second half, separately. A fitted one changes its name every time you cut the data.

10. How do I know? By this. By the count, not by the feeling.

## The Story

One Sunday I tried to find a single trade that showed what a rule was doing, and I could not.

I wanted it for a note I was writing to myself. The rule had been in a forward test for some time, frozen, filled on paper each day, and I wanted to put one example at the top of the note: here is a trade, here is the edge, this is what the rule sees. It seemed a small thing to want.

I went through the trades one at a time. A good one first, naturally. It had gone up, roughly as the rule hoped. Then I opened the random control for the same week, the book of the same number of names picked by dice, and found a name the dice had picked that had done much the same thing for no reason at all. I tried another good trade. The dice had one of those too. I tried a bad trade, thinking it might at least show the rule losing in an interesting way. It looked like a bad pick from the dice.

After an hour I gave up. There was no trade that showed the edge, because the edge was not in any trade. Each one was mostly noise, with a little of whatever the rule knew stirred in, far too little to see. If the rule had anything, it would show only in the count: many trades against many dice, over a long time, the shape coming slowly out of the fog.

*The way, as a thing, is elusive and indistinct. Yet within it there is an image.* I put no example at the top of the note. I wrote that there was none.

---

That was the dim half of the chapter. The other half arrived a few weeks later, and it is about the ledger.

On my desk a language model reads the results each night and proposes what to try next. One morning it proposed an idea I liked very much. It had a fresh name and a clean description. It watched for a certain kind of quiet in a name before a move, and it described that quiet in a way I had not seen put before. I read it twice and thought: this is new.

Before any idea is tested it is written into the ledger, and the ledger asks what family it belongs to. The family is not decided by the name. It is decided by what the test actually does: which prices it reads, what it measures, what makes it buy. The name is a label for people. The ledger looks underneath it.

Underneath, the new idea was old. Years before, I had tried almost the same thing under a different name. Not once. Many times, many ways, over most of one winter, and the ledger had kept every attempt: what was tried, on what data, and that it had failed. I had forgotten nearly all of it. I remembered the winter. I did not remember that the winter had been about this.

*From ancient times until now its name has not gone.* The model had given the idea a new name. The ledger had kept the old one.

---

I want to be honest about the next few minutes, because they are why this story is in the book.

I could have started a new family. The description was different. The model had written it differently, and nobody but me would ever look closely enough to object. A new family starts with a count of one, and an idea with a count of one has an ordinary bar to clear. Attached to the old family, it would carry every one of that winter's attempts, and the bar would be far higher, because an idea tried that many ways will throw up something pretty by luck.

That is exactly why the failed trials have to stay. If I had kept only what worked, the winter would have left nothing in the ledger at all, and the new idea would have walked in looking like a first try. It was not a first try. It was the next try in a long line, and the honest bar was the one that knew the whole line was there.

I attached it to the old family. The count came with it.

It went through the gate with that count beside it. I will not say what happened there. The point is not the outcome. The point is that whatever the gate said, it would be saying it about the right question: not whether this idea looks good, but whether it looks good after this many looks.

---

A ledger that keeps only the winners is a brochure. I have seen many brochures. They are beautiful, and none of them tells you how many pages were left out.

The ledger on my desk is not beautiful. Most of its entries are failures, written down plainly, in the order they happened, with the reason beside each. When I scroll through it I do not see a record of my research. I see a record of my looking: every time I went into the dark with a lamp, and what the lamp showed, and how often it showed nothing.

*The essence is very real, and within it there is proof.* Proof, for a trader, is two numbers kept together: how many times you looked, and what you found. Either one alone is a story. The found thing without the count is a brochure. The count without the found thing is a diary.

On the Sunday I could not find a trade to put at the top of the note, I thought the dimness was a failure of the rule. I think now it was the rule being honest. An edge you can see in one trade is a story about one trade. An edge you can see only in the count, after every look has been counted, is the only kind that has ever been worth the name.

*How do I know what the beginning of all things is like? By this.*
