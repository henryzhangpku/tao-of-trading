# 53. The Great Way Is Smooth

*Part II: The Virtue · 《使我介然有知》*

## The Verse

使我介然有知，行于大道，唯施是畏。大道甚夷，而民好径。

朝甚除，田甚芜，仓甚虚；服文彩，带利剑，厌饮食，财货有余；是谓盗夸。非道也哉！

If I had even a little knowledge, walking on the great way, the only thing I would fear is straying from it. The great way is very level, but people love side paths.

The court is swept very clean, the fields are choked with weeds, the granaries are empty. They wear embroidered silk, carry sharp swords, are glutted with food and drink, and have wealth to spare. This is called the boasting of robbers. It is not the way!

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## What You Know

Work smarter, not harder. Every success story you were told had the same turn in it: someone found the faster way, the shortcut, the trick the others missed. Waiting in the long queue is for people who did not think. Trading rewards cleverness above all. Why grind through years of small, plain trades when one good tip, a position copied from someone who knows, or a cheap option on the right name can do in a week what the slow road does in a year? The main road is crowded and slow. The money is on the path nobody else has noticed yet.

## The Other Way

1. *The great way is very level, but people love side paths.* The level road is boring because it is level. Nothing on it promises to get you there early.

2. At the desk the level road is plain: a size you can afford to lose, an exit written down, the kind of trade you know, repeated. You already know it. That is exactly why it does not tempt you.

3. A side path looks shorter because you cannot see where it ends.

4. *The only thing I would fear is straying.* The danger is not the dull stretch of road. It is the turn-off you take on a dull afternoon.

5. A tip is somebody else's entry with none of their reasons, none of their size and none of their exit. You get the start of a trade and have to make up the rest while it moves.

6. Straying is rarely one big leap. It is a small step off the road that looks harmless, and then another step to get back.

7. *The court is swept very clean, the fields are choked with weeds.* Look at what you tend. Three screens, paid data, a tidy watchlist, and no written record of how your own plain trades have gone.

8. *The granaries are empty.* The fields are the dull work that feeds you: writing the trade down, looking at it again, the same routine every morning. Leave them for the clever thing and nothing is stored when the lean month comes.

9. *They wear embroidered silk and carry sharp swords.* The side path always comes well dressed: a screenshot, a confident voice, a big number. The dress is the only part you can see.

10. *This is called the boasting of robbers. It is not the way!* A gain from a side path teaches you to look for the next side path. That lesson costs more than the gain was worth.

## The Story

It is a slow Monday. The names you trade are doing nothing. The one plain trade you are waiting for, a pullback in a stock you know well toward a level you marked last week, is not there yet. It may come this afternoon. It may not come at all. You have spent three weeks taking small, careful trades, and the account has barely moved.

At twenty past eleven someone in a chat you follow posts a small company with a single line: about to run. Underneath is a screenshot of his win from last Friday. It is the most interesting thing that has happened all morning. It feels like the clever way through a dull month.

You buy at 3.40. The price is low, so the share count is high, bigger than anything you usually hold. By eleven forty it is 3.55 and you feel clever. Then you realise you do not know where you would sell. You ask in the chat. Somebody says to hold for five.

---

By one it is 3.10. The man who posted it says this is a shakeout of weak hands. You do not want to be a weak hand, so you hold. You watch it every minute. You have no reason of your own to hold it or to sell it, so every tick asks you the question again.

At a quarter past two your stock, the plain one, pulls back to your level. It sits there for twenty minutes and turns up. You see it out of the corner of your eye and do nothing, because all of you is on the small company. By the close the plain trade has gone quietly the way you expected. The small company is at 2.80. You sell it in the last minute.

That evening your three screens are still glowing. You open the notebook where you used to write down every trade. The last entry is from Thursday. *The court is swept very clean, the fields are choked with weeds.* Everything you can see looks like a serious desk. The work that would have fed you was left to go to seed.

---

Now the other Monday.

The same slow morning, the same plain trade not yet there. At twenty past eleven the same post appears. You feel the pull in your stomach, a little lift. You notice it. You have a separate sheet for exactly this, labelled side paths. You write the name, the time and where it came from. You do not buy it.

Then you go back to the dull road. You make a cup of tea. You look at your one level and nothing else.

At a quarter past two the stock pulls back to your level, sits there, and turns. You buy it at the size you always use, with the stop written down before the order. It moves a little your way by the close. Nothing to tell anyone about.

After the close you write the trade into the notebook, as you do every day. Then you look at the side-paths sheet. The small company ran and then fell. On another day it might have run and kept running. Either way, you never knew where it ended, and you would have had to find out with your money.

---

*The great way is very level, but people love side paths.* We are taught that the clever person finds the shortcut and only the slow and dull stay on the main road. The old book sees it the other way round. The fear worth having is not of the long flat road. It is of the turn-off that looks shorter.

Most days on the level road look like nothing happened. That is what it looks like.

## Try This

For the next two weeks, keep a separate sheet for side paths. Each time something pulls you off your usual way of trading, write it down with the time and where it came from, and do not act on it: a tip, a post, a product you do not normally trade, a size bigger than usual because it "can't miss". Keep writing up your own plain trades as always. At the end, read the sheet and look at what each side path would have needed from you that you did not have: a reason, a size, an exit.
