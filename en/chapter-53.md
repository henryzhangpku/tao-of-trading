# 53. The Great Way Is Smooth

*Part II: The Virtue · 《使我介然有知》*

## The Verse

使我介然有知，行于大道，唯施是畏。大道甚夷，而民好径。

朝甚除，田甚芜，仓甚虚；服文彩，带利剑，厌饮食，财货有余；是谓盗夸。非道也哉！

If I had even a little knowledge, walking on the great way, the only thing I would fear is straying from it. The great way is very level, but people love side paths.

The court is swept very clean, the fields are choked with weeds, the granaries are empty. They wear embroidered silk, carry sharp swords, are glutted with food and drink, and have wealth to spare. This is called the boasting of robbers. It is not the way!

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## The Trader

1. The great way is level: small size, a written stop, a tested rule, a long count. It is flat and dull, and it goes somewhere.

2. People love side paths: the tip, the sure thing, the position copied from someone louder.

3. A side path looks shorter because you cannot see where it ends.

4. The only thing to fear is straying. The danger is not the hard stretch of the road. It is the turn-off.

5. Copying a famous trader copies the entry and nothing else. Not the size, not the stop, not the reason, not the exit. You get the position without the plan.

6. If a public voice is worth following, it is worth logging first. Every call written down on the day it is made, misses beside hits, before a single trade follows it. Most voices do not survive their own count.

7. The court swept clean, the fields full of weeds: the handsome screen, the expensive data, the confident videos, and a ledger nobody has opened.

8. Embroidered silk and sharp swords: the account that looks rich because it is borrowed.

9. The boasting of robbers is a claimed gain with no record behind it. Distrust any boast you cannot audit, including your own.

10. It is not the way. It is only a faster road back to the start.

## The Story

There is an investor I will not name, because the name is not the point and because he is real. He is well known, he talks in public often, and he says what he is buying. For a while it seemed that half the people I knew who traded were following him. Not loosely. They would see a post in the evening and own the name by the next open.

A friend asked me one night whether I followed him too. I said I did not. She asked why not, if he was so often right. I realised that I had no idea whether he was often right. Neither did she. Neither, I suspect, did most of the people buying at the open. Everyone remembered his famous calls. Nobody had counted the others.

*The great way is very level, but people love side paths.* Copying him was a side path, and it looked very short.

---

So I did what I do with any idea that arrives on my desk sounding good. I put it in the ledger before believing it. The trial was not *should I copy him*. It was narrower and duller: *what do his public calls do, counted honestly, from today?*

Counting honestly turned out to be most of the work.

The first problem was deciding what a call was. People who talk in public rarely say *buy this at this price and sell it on this day*. They say they like something. They say a sector is interesting. They say a name is cheap, and three weeks later that it is even cheaper. Before I could log anything I had to write rules for what counted, and the rules had to be written before the first entry, so that I could not choose them afterwards to make the count look a particular way. A call was a named instrument and a direction, said in public, with a date. The log took the price at the next open after the call, because that was the first price a follower could really have had. It held each call for a fixed horizon, the same for every call, written down in advance. If he changed his mind in public before the horizon, that went in the log too, as a second call.

The second problem was beside it. Every call went into the log next to a random control: the same number of names, chosen by dice, entered on the same days, held for the same horizon. A good market makes everyone look clever. The control is there so that it cannot make him look cleverer than the dice.

The third problem was me.

---

Some weeks in, he made a call that I liked. It was in a part of the market I knew, the reasoning was clean, and the name had the kind of chart that makes you feel you have arrived just in time. Every part of me that had ever enjoyed being early wanted to buy it the next morning, at small size, just this once, while the log went on counting.

I did not. The count was not long enough to say anything, and I had written that down too, in the trial itself: the number of calls the log needed before I would read it at all. A count of a few weeks is not a record. It is an anecdote with a date column. And I noticed something else. If I followed this one call, I would have his entry and nothing else. Not his size, not his stop, not the reason he would leave. I would be holding his position without his plan, which is the most dangerous way there is to hold anything.

*The only thing I would fear is straying.* The danger was not the long count. It was the turn-off halfway down it.

---

The log ran its length. I am not going to tell you what it showed, about him or about the dice. It would be a claim about someone else's results, which is no more mine to make than a claim about my own. What I can tell you is the thing that surprised me, and it was not in the count. It was beside it.

At the end of the log's season he gave a long interview looking back on the year. He listed his calls. The list was handsome. It was also not the list in my ledger. Some calls I had logged were not in it. Some calls in it were described a little differently from the way I had written them down on the day. I do not think he was lying. I think he was remembering, which is what all of us do, and memory is an editor that only works in one direction.

*This is called the boasting of robbers.* That line is harsher than I would be about anyone in particular. But the shape it describes is exact: a court swept clean, fields full of weeds. A handsome account of gains, and no record behind it that anyone has opened.

---

The last thing I did was turn the ledger round.

I talk in public too, sometimes, about ideas and rules, though never about what I hold. So I set up the same log for myself, with the same rules: what counts as a call, the next open, the fixed horizon, the random twin, written down before the first entry. The desk keeps it. I do not get to edit it, and I do not get to remember it. When I want to know what I said, I read what I said.

*Distrust any boast you cannot audit, including your own.* It turns out I can audit mine, and the audit is very dull, and the dullness is the great way. It is level. It goes somewhere. It does not look short, because you can see all of it.
