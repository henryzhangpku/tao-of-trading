# 72. When People Do Not Fear

*Part II: The Virtue · 《民不畏威》*

## The Verse

民不畏威，则大威至。

无狎其所居，无厌其所生。夫唯不厌，是以不厌。

是以圣人自知不自见，自爱不自贵。故去彼取此。

When people no longer fear what is dreadful, the great dread arrives.

Do not crowd them in their dwellings. Do not press on their lives. Only when you do not press on them will they not turn against you.

So the sage knows himself and does not display himself. He cares for himself and does not exalt himself. So he leaves that and takes this.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## The Trader

1. When the trader stops fearing the tail, the tail arrives. Not because the market is watching, but because a trader without fear has already sized for a world without tails.

2. Calm months are when risk grows. The quiet does not reduce the danger. It reduces your memory of it, and size creeps up to fill the space.

3. So keep the protection on in the calm month, when it feels most unnecessary. The stop stays in. The size stays small. The cash stays uncommitted. Protection bought in a storm is bought at the storm's price.

4. Do not crowd the account in its dwelling. Leave room in it: no margin used to the last inch, no position so large that one gap would move into the house.

5. Do not press on its life. An account pushed to work every day, in every market, at full size, will one day push back.

6. Only when you do not press will it not turn on you. The account that is asked for little is the one still standing after the bad week.

7. The sage knows himself and does not display himself. Know your risk in detail. Do not show your returns to anyone, including yourself every hour.

8. He cares for himself and does not exalt himself. Protect yourself as if you were ordinary, because in the tail everyone is. Nobody is the exception in a crash.

9. Leave that, take this: leave the feeling that the calm will last; take the stop that assumes it will not.

## The Story

This is the chapter where Mark Spitznagel's *The Dao of Capital* and this book stand closest, and I should say so before the story rather than after it. Protection against the rare, violent fall — the tail — is near the centre of his work, and the idea that it is bought when nobody wants it, rather than when it is needed, was his long before it was mine. He thinks about it at the scale of a portfolio and across decades. What follows is smaller: one line on one trader's budget, in one quiet month.

---

The line is on a sheet where I keep the account's standing costs. Most of the lines are what you would expect: data, the machines that run at night, fees. One is different. It says *protection*, and beside it there is a rule.

The rule is simple, and it was written on a calm weekend long before the month in this story. A small, fixed share of the budget is spent on a schedule, every period, on protection: options that pay only if the market falls hard and fast, and are otherwise worth nothing when they expire. The amount is fixed. The schedule is fixed. Whether I feel like it is not an input.

Most periods the protection expires worthless. That is what it is for. It is a cost you pay so that a particular kind of morning cannot reach the house.

---

The month in this story was the calmest I can remember. The market went up a little on most days and down a little on the others, and nothing moved far enough to make anyone look up. The measures of expected movement, which the price of protection depends on, sank week by week. Conversations about markets drifted to other subjects. Nobody I spoke to mentioned risk at all.

It was also the month in which the protection line looked most foolish.

In the third week I found myself, on a Sunday afternoon, opening the sheet and looking at that line for longer than it deserved. Every other line on the sheet bought something I could see. The data came in. The machines ran. The protection line bought nothing, every period, and had bought nothing for a long time. It was the only line on the sheet that only cost.

I did what I have learned to do when I want to change a rule: I wrote out the case in full, as if I were proposing it to someone else. *Expected movement is low and falling. The protection has expired worthless for many periods running. The money could stay in the account. Pause it until conditions change, then resume.*

Written down, it was persuasive. It was also, I noticed, written in exactly the voice this chapter warns about.

---

*When people no longer fear what is dreadful, the great dread arrives.* I had always read that as a line about rulers and the ruled. That Sunday I read it as a description of my sheet.

The case for pausing was built entirely out of calm. Every reason in it was a reason the month had been quiet. None was a reason the next month would be. And the one thing the calm had really changed — the price of protection, lower than it had been in a long time — pointed the other way. The month in which I most wanted to stop paying was the month in which the payment bought the most.

There was a quieter reason too, and it took me longer to see. The protection was not only a position. It was what allowed everything else in the account to be sized the way it was. Every other rule I trade had been sized on the assumption that a particular kind of morning could not wipe the account out, and that assumption rested on that one line. Pausing it would not have been a small saving. It would have changed what every other position meant, without changing any of them.

*Do not crowd them in their dwellings.* Without the line, the account would have been living closer to the wall than it knew.

---

So I left it. No pause, no smaller amount, no clever version that bought less when the market was calm. The rule is a rule, and if I wanted to change it, the desk's way is to write the change as a new rule and test it forward, with its own fingerprint, for a season, beside the old one. I did not write it. I closed the sheet.

The protection bought that month expired, like most of it, worth nothing. I paid for it and received what it was for: during that month and the next, no morning could have reached the house through that door. That is not a result. It is the purchase.

I am not going to tell you what happened in the months after. If a storm had come, the story would look like a prophecy, and it was not one. If none came, it would look like a waste, and it was not that either. The rule was not written to be right about any particular month. It was written so that I would never have to decide, in a calm month, whether I was still afraid.

*The sage knows himself and does not display himself.* I know that in a calm month I stop being afraid. It is the most useful thing I know about myself as a trader, and the protection line is where I keep it.
