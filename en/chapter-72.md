# 72. When People Do Not Fear

*Part II: The Virtue · 《民不畏威》*

## The Verse

民不畏威，则大威至。

无狎其所居，无厌其所生。夫唯不厌，是以不厌。

是以圣人自知不自见，自爱不自贵。故去彼取此。

When people no longer fear what is dreadful, the great dread arrives.

Do not crowd them in their dwellings. Do not press on their lives. Only when you do not press on them will they not turn against you.

So the sage knows himself and does not display himself. He cares for himself and does not exalt himself. So he leaves that and takes this.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## What You Know

Fear is the enemy. Every trading book says so, and so does every coach and every speech about success: face your fears and push through them. In trading, fear makes you sell winners too early, freeze at the entry, miss the move. The best traders have trained it out of themselves. They look at a position that could cost them a month and feel nothing. That calm is the goal. When you stop being afraid of losing, you can finally trade the way you were meant to: freely, with size, without flinching. Fear of loss is a weakness, and you are working on it.

## The Other Way

1. *When people no longer fear what is dreadful, the great dread arrives.* The day you stop being afraid of a position is the day it can do the most damage.

2. Fear is not the enemy of trading. It is the part of you that measures. Turn it off and nothing measures.

3. There are two fears. One is the jumpy fear of every tick. The other is a plain respect for what could happen. Let go of the first and keep the second.

4. A long winning run takes fear away quietly. That is when size creeps up without anyone deciding it should.

5. *Do not crowd them in their dwellings.* Do not trade with the money you live on: the rent, the school fees, the cushion. A trader squeezed at home trades badly at the desk.

6. *Do not press on their lives.* Do not press on your own life either. A trader who demands a fixed sum from the market every month is pressing on himself, and it will turn against him.

7. *The sage knows himself.* Know how much you can lose before you stop thinking clearly. It is less than you think.

8. *And does not display himself.* The trader who acts fearless for an audience is the one most likely to stop being afraid in private as well.

9. *He cares for himself and does not exalt himself.* Care: rest, sleep, smaller size on a bad day. Do not exalt: no belief that you are the kind of trader things do not happen to.

10. *He leaves that and takes this.* Leave the fearless pose. Keep the small, steady fear that makes you check the size once more before you send.

## The Story

It has been a good two months. Not spectacular, just steady: more good weeks than bad, no loss that kept you up at night. Somewhere in those two months something happened that you did not notice. You stopped being afraid.

It shows only in small ways. You used to check your size twice before sending; now you check it once. You used to close most positions before an earnings report or a big announcement; now you hold some through, because the last few were fine. Your usual size has grown a little at a time, without any day on which you decided to grow it. When you look at your open positions in the evening, you feel calm. You have read that calm is what good traders feel.

Then one morning a position you held overnight opens far against you, on news nobody saw coming. The loss is larger than any you have taken in a year, and it happened before you could do anything. Looking back, every piece of it had been there for weeks: the larger size, the holding through events, the one check instead of two. None of it felt dangerous, because the part of you that would have felt it had gone quiet.

*When people no longer fear what is dreadful, the great dread arrives.* The great dread did not come from the market. It was the same market as always. It came in through the door your fear used to guard.

---

There is another way to read the books that tell you to conquer fear. What they mean is the jumpy fear: the one that makes you dump a good position on the first red tick, that keeps you out of a setup you have taken a hundred times. That fear is worth working on. It belongs to the tick, not to the risk.

But there is a second fear under it, slower and quieter. It is the fear that knows a gap can happen, that size can kill, that the cushion in your account is the cushion in your life. That fear is not a weakness. It is the part of you that keeps the account alive while the rest of you is busy trading.

So after a good two months, you check on it the way you would check a smoke alarm. Is my size where I meant it to be, or where it drifted? Am I holding things through events I used to respect? Is any of this money I need for the house? *Do not crowd them in their dwellings.* If the trading account has started to lean on the money you live on, that is the first thing to move back.

And you keep this to yourself. *The sage knows himself and does not display himself.* You do not need to look calm to anyone. A little fear before you send an order, a second look at the size, a smaller position the night before an announcement: nobody sees these, and nobody needs to. You care for yourself and do not exalt yourself. You are not the kind of trader things do not happen to. Nobody is.

---

So the old advice turns over. We are told the brave feel no fear. Laozi says the one who keeps a little fear is the one who stays safe, and the one who has lost it is the one in danger. The good months can go on; they usually do, for a while. What changes is that when the morning comes that opens far against you, the position is the size you chose, and you knew it was there.

## Try This

At the end of each good week this month, spend five minutes on a smoke-alarm check: compare your average size to what you meant it to be, list anything you are holding through an event, and confirm that none of the account is money you need to live on. Write the answers down. Notice what has drifted.
