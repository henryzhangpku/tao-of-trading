# 35. Hold the Great Image

*Part I: The Way · 《执大象》*

## The Verse

执大象，天下往。往而不害，安平太。

乐与饵，过客止。道之出口，淡乎其无味，视之不足见，听之不足闻，用之不足既。

Hold the great image, and the world comes to you. It comes and comes to no harm: safety, peace and calm.

Music and good food make the passing traveller stop. But the way, when it leaves the mouth, is bland and has no flavour. Look at it, and there is not enough to see. Listen to it, and there is not enough to hear. Use it, and it cannot be used up.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## What You Know

A trade worth taking has a story worth telling. You want to know the catalyst, the theme, the reason this one could run for months. Conviction comes from a story you can feel in your chest, and the trades that make real money usually start as ideas you cannot wait to tell someone about. If a setup bores you, there is probably nothing in it. Good traders have taste. They can tell the live idea from the dead one, and they are drawn to the live one. Excitement is information. It means you have found something the market has not priced yet. It is how you were taught to choose almost everything: follow what excites you.

## The Other Way

1. Music and bait make the traveller stop. The story, the theme, the friend's voice on the phone: these are what stop you. They are not why the price moves.

2. The way, when it leaves the mouth, has no flavour. Describe your best setup out loud. If it sounds dull, you have probably described it correctly.

3. Bland is not empty. The thrilling fades and the bland lasts. A plain setup gives you nothing to fall in love with, and so nothing to defend when it fails.

4. *Look at it, and there is not enough to see.* A level, a size, a place to be wrong, a place to leave. That is all a good trade usually is.

5. *Listen to it, and there is not enough to hear.* A trade taken well makes very little noise, in the room or in you.

6. The exciting trade wants you to tell someone. The bland trade wants nothing from you. Notice which one you are taking.

7. Taste is a dangerous edge. What thrills you has already thrilled the people who bought before you.

8. *Use it, and it cannot be used up.* The dull trade can be done again tomorrow, the same way. The thrilling one is a story, and a story can only be told once.

9. Hold the great image, and the world comes. Keep to the plain thing, and the trades come on their own schedule.

10. It comes, and comes to no harm. Choose the trading you could do every day for twenty years without needing to be excited.

## The Story

It is the evening, and the phone lights up. A friend who trades, the one who always seems to be onto something. He has found a name: a small company, a new product, a contract nobody has noticed, a heavy short interest that could make it run. He talks fast. You can hear in his voice that he has already bought.

You open the chart while he talks. It has moved, but not too much. The story is good. It has every ingredient: something new, something hidden, something that could double. By the time you hang up you have a plan for the open, and you feel something you have not felt in weeks. You feel awake.

Your own setup, the one you have traded for years, is on the other screen. It is a pullback to a level in something large and liquid. You have taken it a hundred times. You could take it in your sleep, and that is exactly the trouble with it. Nobody calls you in the evening about it. There is nothing to say about it except where you would buy and where you would be wrong.

---

At the open you buy the story. You take more than you usually would, because this one could really go. It opens strong, and for twenty minutes you are right, and the feeling is very good. You check your friend's messages. He is excited too.

Then it stops going up. It does not crash; it simply drifts, the way stories drift when the people who heard them first begin to sell to the people who heard them second. You hold, because the story has not changed. The story never changes. That is what a story is. By the afternoon you are down, you are still holding, and you are reading the company's website for the third time, looking for the thing that will make it run.

Meanwhile, on the other screen, your dull pullback came to its level an hour after the open, held, and went up quietly for the rest of the day. You did not take it. You were busy.

---

Look at what stopped you. *Music and good food make the passing traveller stop.* It was not the price. It was the flavour: the hidden contract, the possible squeeze, your friend's voice. Every one of those was real. None of them told you where the buyers would be tomorrow. The story was seasoning, and you made a meal of the seasoning.

The setup you skipped had no flavour at all. That is why it works. Nobody gets excited about it, so nobody crowds into it, so it still behaves the way it behaved last year. You cannot fall in love with it, so you do not hold it past the point where it is wrong. When it fails you lose a small amount, and you do not think about it at dinner.

*Look at it, and there is not enough to see.* The bland trade gives the eye almost nothing. Four lines on a card. A trader who needs his trading to be interesting will always find it too thin.

---

There is another way to take that phone call.

Your friend talks, and you listen, and you enjoy it. It is a good story, and good stories are one of the pleasures of this work. You say it sounds interesting. You put the name on a list and do nothing with it in the morning, because nothing about it looks like the trade you know how to take.

At the open you watch your dull pullback. It comes to the level and holds, and you buy the size you always buy, with the price where you would be wrong written down. It goes up quietly. You sell where you sell. Nothing happened that you could tell anyone about.

That night your friend calls again. The story stock gave back its gains, and he is still in. He asks what you did today, and you tell him, and you can hear him lose interest halfway through the sentence. That is how you know you described it correctly.

*Use it, and it cannot be used up.* The story will be gone in a week and a new one will take its place. The plain setup will be there tomorrow at a different level, and you will take it the same way.

## Try This

This week, before each trade, say it out loud in one sentence: where you buy, where you are wrong, where you leave. If you hear yourself adding the story, stop and ask whether you would take the trade without it. Watch which trades you still want.
