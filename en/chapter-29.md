# 29. The World Cannot Be Forced

*Part I: The Way · 《将欲取天下而为之》*

## The Verse

将欲取天下而为之，吾见其不得已。天下神器，不可为也。为者败之，执者失之。

故物或行或随，或歔或吹，或强或羸，或挫或隳。是以圣人去甚，去奢，去泰。

Whoever wants to take the world and work on it: I see that he will not get there. The world is a sacred vessel. It cannot be worked on. Whoever works on it ruins it. Whoever grips it loses it.

So among things some lead and some follow, some breathe gently and some blow hard, some are strong and some are frail, some are broken and some fall. So the sage puts away the extreme, the extravagant and the excessive.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## What You Know

Take control of your results. That is what you were told at school, at work, in every talk about getting on in life: nobody is coming to save you, so own the outcome. A professional does not wait for good luck; he makes his month. Trading is a business, and a business has steady income. You know roughly what you need each week, and a good trader brings it in, in quiet markets and loud ones. If the month is behind, you work harder, find more trades, push a little more size. Amateurs let the market decide what they earn. Professionals decide for themselves.

## The Other Way

1. *Whoever wants to take the world and work on it: I see that he will not get there.* You choose your orders. The market chooses what it pays.

2. *The world is a sacred vessel.* Not holy. Only not yours to shape. It was here before your number for the month and will be here after it.

3. *Whoever works on it ruins it.* The trade forced to meet a target is the worst trade of the month, and it always looks reasonable on the day.

4. *Whoever grips it loses it.* The tighter you hold the month, the more of it slips through your hands.

5. *Some lead and some follow.* Some weeks the market moves and you go with it. Some weeks nothing leads, and there is nothing to follow.

6. *Some breathe gently and some blow hard.* A gentle market will not pay a hard market's wage. Asking it to is how a quiet week becomes a bad one.

7. *Some are strong and some are frail.* Your own seasons are the same. A frail stretch is a season, not a fault to be fixed by Friday.

8. A salary is paid by someone who promised it. Nobody promised you anything.

9. *Put away the extreme, the extravagant and the excessive.* The sage does not take away the market's extremes. He takes away his own: the extra size, the extra trade, the extra hour.

10. Control is real, and it is small: what you risk, when you stop, what you leave alone. Hold that, and let go of the rest.

## The Story

It is the third week of the month and you are behind. You have a number in your head, the amount a decent month should bring in, and so far you have about a third of it. The market has been gentle for weeks. The index drifts a little up, a little down. Names that used to move two dollars a day move forty cents. The setups you know barely appear, and when they do, they go nowhere.

On Tuesday morning you sit down with the number. Seven sessions left. You do the sum, as you always do, and it tells you how much you need per day. It is more than you usually make on a good day.

So you take charge. You take a setup that is a little worse than your usual ones, because a little worse is better than nothing. You size it up, because the moves are small and you need more shares to make them count. It chops back and forth, and you close it for a small loss. Now the number is larger. In the afternoon you find something in a name you rarely trade, which is at least moving, and take that too. You hold it overnight, which you do not normally do, because the day did not give you enough time.

By Friday you have traded twice as often as in the first two weeks. You are further behind than when you started. You are also tired in a way that the quiet market does not explain. You spent the week working on the market, and it did not notice.

---

Now the same third week, and the same number behind you.

You look at the month on Tuesday morning and see what it is: a gentle month. Nothing leads, so there is nothing to follow. You do not do the sum. You notice the urge to do it, and you leave the pencil where it is.

You trade the way the market is trading. Your usual setups, at your usual size, or smaller, because a market with small moves can still hurt a large position. When nothing appears, you do not go looking in names you do not know. Two days pass with no trades at all. It feels like failing at your job. You let it feel like that.

On Thursday a setup you know turns up, plain and ordinary, and you take it. It makes a little. On Friday you take nothing. The month ends behind the number. That is the whole story of the month.

The next month the market wakes up. Moves get wider, and your setups come back. You are rested, your size is normal, and you have no hole to dig out of, because you never dug one. Some of what the quiet month did not pay, the loud one does. Not all of it, and not on schedule.

---

*Whoever works on it ruins it. Whoever grips it loses it.* We were taught that a serious person takes control: set the goal, make it happen, and treat any shortfall as a failure of effort. The old book sees it the other way round. The world has its own seasons, some leading and some following, some strong and some frail. The part you can hold is your own extremes, and you put those away.

Some months pay little. They do not owe you the difference.

## Try This

For the next month, keep your monthly number out of sight. Do not write it at the top of the page and do not divide it into days. Instead, each time you feel behind, write one line saying what you were about to do to catch up: the bigger size, the unfamiliar name, the overnight hold. Do not do it. At the end of the month, read the list and look at how many of those trades you would have taken in a month that was going well.
