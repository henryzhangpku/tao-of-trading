# 4. The Empty Vessel

*Part I: The Way · 《道冲》*

## The Verse

道冲，而用之或不盈。渊兮，似万物之宗。

挫其锐，解其纷，和其光，同其尘。

湛兮，似或存。吾不知谁之子，象帝之先。

The way is empty, and using it never fills it. How deep it is, like the ancestor of the ten thousand things.

It blunts the sharp and unties the tangled. It softens the glare and settles with the dust.

How still and clear, as if it were barely there. I do not know whose child it is. It seems to come before the first ancestor.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## What You Know

Idle money is wasted money. You were taught that about everything: fill the calendar, use the whole day, put every dollar to work. A trader with cash on the sidelines is a trader who is not making anything. So you stay invested. You have positions on most days, and when one closes, you look for the next one to take its place. You use the buying power you have, because the broker gave it to you for a reason. The market goes up over time, and the only way to catch the move is to be in it.

## The Other Way

1. *The way is empty, and using it never fills it.* The account is useful because of what is not yet in it. Cash is not the absence of a position. It is every position you can still take.

2. A full account has already made all its decisions. When the real chance comes, it has nothing left to say.

3. *How deep it is.* Depth is reserve. The trader with room can sit through a bad week; the trader without it is sitting on a ledge.

4. *It blunts the sharp.* Full size makes every tick sharp. Half the size, and the same tick is only information.

5. *It unties the tangled.* Five positions on at once tie themselves together. You close one to pay for another, and soon you are trading your positions instead of the market.

6. *It softens the glare.* A full account glares at you all day. You cannot look away from it, so you cannot see anything else.

7. *It settles with the dust.* Being out is ordinary. Nobody admires it. That is why so few can bear it.

8. Buying power is not an invitation. It is the size of the hole you are allowed to dig.

9. *As if it were barely there.* The best part of a good account is the part that is never used, and is there every day.

10. Empty is useful. A bowl is a bowl because of the space inside it.

## The Story

It is a Tuesday, and you are fully in. Four positions, each one reasonable on its own. You took the last one yesterday afternoon because the third had closed and the cash looked lazy sitting there. Your buying power line reads close to zero. It feels like being busy, and being busy feels like working. You check the account more often than you need to, because there is so much in it to check.

At ten in the morning the market drops hard. Not on anything you can find; it simply drops, two percent in half an hour, the kind of flush you have been waiting weeks to see. One of the names you most wanted to own trades straight into the level you marked a month ago. This is the trade you have been describing to yourself for a month.

You have nothing to buy it with.

---

So you do what a full account makes you do. You look at your four positions to see which one to sell. All four are down, because the whole market is down. You pick the one that is down least and sell it into the flush, at a price you would never have chosen, to make room. By the time the order fills and the cash settles in your head, the name you wanted has bounced off the level without you. You chase it a little higher, with less size than you meant, and now you have sold low and bought high on the same morning.

That evening you look at the account. Every position you held was a fine idea. Together they made you blind and slow at the one moment that mattered. *It unties the tangled.* Yours were knotted together so tightly that moving one meant breaking another.

The full account also had a quieter cost, before the flush ever came. For weeks you had watched four positions all day. Every tick in each was a small pull. You were never calm enough to see the market as a whole, because a part of you was always standing guard over what you held. *It softens the glare.* Yours glared at you from the moment you woke.

---

Now the other Tuesday.

You come in with two positions and most of the account in cash. It does not feel good. Cash never does. The market went up yesterday and you were half in it. A voice says you are leaving money on the table, and the voice is right. You let it be right.

At ten the market drops two percent. Your two positions are down. It stings a little, and it does not matter much, because they are small. You look at the name you wanted. It trades into your level. You buy it, at the size you planned, with money that was waiting for exactly this. You did not have to sell anything. You did not have to decide which of your children to give up. Nothing in your head is tangled. You see the trade, and you see what you will do if it fails, and that is all there is to see.

The rest of the day is quiet. If the trade works, good. If it fails, you are out at the price you wrote down, and there is still cash in the account for the next real chance.

---

*The way is empty, and using it never fills it.* We are taught that fullness is strength: a full schedule, a full account, every hour and every dollar spoken for. The old book sees it the other way round. The empty part is the useful part. The room you keep is what lets you move when moving matters, and lets you sit still when it does not.

On most days the cash does nothing at all. You will have to be able to live with that.

## Try This

For one week, set a ceiling on how much of the account may be in positions, well below your buying power, and keep the rest in cash every day, whatever the market does. When the cash feels lazy, write down the feeling instead of filling it. At the end of the week, look at whether a chance came that you could only take because the room was there.
