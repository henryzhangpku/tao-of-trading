# 4. The Empty Vessel

*Part I: The Way · 《道冲》*

## The Verse

道冲，而用之或不盈。渊兮，似万物之宗。

挫其锐，解其纷，和其光，同其尘。

湛兮，似或存。吾不知谁之子，象帝之先。

The way is empty, and using it never fills it. How deep it is, like the ancestor of the ten thousand things.

It blunts the sharp and unties the tangled. It softens the glare and settles with the dust.

How still and clear, as if it were barely there. I do not know whose child it is. It seems to come before the first ancestor.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## The Trader

1. A bowl is useful because it is empty. An account is useful because part of it is. Cash is a position.

2. Unused buying power is not waste. It is capacity: the room the next good trade will need, and the room the next bad week will take.

3. A vessel that is full cannot receive anything. A fully committed account cannot act on the one setup that matters.

4. Use it, and do not fill it. Size so that a whole day of losses leaves the bowl mostly empty.

5. Blunt the sharp: the urge to act because the screen is moving.

6. Untie the tangled. When positions are many and all depend on the same thing, they are one position wearing different names.

7. Soften the glare: the ticker, the alert, the headline. Light is not information.

8. Settle with the dust. On most days nothing of yours needs to happen.

9. The trades you did not take leave no trace in the account. Write them down, or you will never know that they were the work.

10. Deep and still, as if barely there. From outside, a good week can look as if nobody was home.

## The Story

There is a kind of week that never appears in trading books, because there is nothing in it to write about. Nothing happened. The account was flat on Monday and flat on Friday. No entries. No exits, because there was nothing to exit. A statement for that week would be a blank page.

I want to write about one of those weeks, because it was one of the hardest I have had.

---

The rules I trade run a funnel each morning. Thousands of names go in at the top. Most are thrown out for plain reasons before anything interesting is asked of them: the market in them is too thin, the spread too wide, a report due too soon, the price too erratic to size against. What is left is asked whether it meets the rule. Usually a few do. Some days one does. That week, on five mornings in a row, none did.

The first morning, an empty board feels like a rest. The second, it feels like a coincidence. By the third it starts to feel like a fault, as if the funnel were broken, or too strict, as if I had built something that had stopped working without telling me. I checked the logs. The funnel was working exactly as written. Names had come in, names had been refused, each one for a stated reason. There was simply nothing that cleared.

On the fourth morning I caught myself doing something I now recognise as the beginning of most bad trades. I was going through the names the funnel had refused, one by one, asking which of them almost qualified.

---

There is always one that almost qualifies. That is what *almost* means. A chart that nearly broke out. A spread a little too wide, but only a little. A setup that would have passed last season's version of the rule, before I tightened something. Each one comes with its own reason, and each reason is reasonable.

I had a name in front of me that was one condition short. I had a size in mind. I noticed I had already decided where the stop would go, which is the moment you know you have stopped asking *whether* and started asking *how*.

What stopped me was not willpower. It was a habit that came out of a short book I wrote years ago about doing less: a trade that is not in the plan does not go out on the day it is thought of. It waits a day. If it is still good tomorrow, it can become a rule and be tested like one. Almost nothing survives the wait, and that is what the wait is for.

So I wrote the name down instead. Not as a trade. As a refusal: the name, what it was missing, and the reason I wanted it anyway. The reason, written plainly, was that the week had been empty and I did not like how that felt.

That sentence has done more work for me than any filter I have built.

---

*The way is empty, and using it never fills it.* Laozi's image in this chapter is a vessel, and the usual reading is that a vessel is useful because of its hollow. A cup that is already full cannot be poured into. I had read the line many times as a thought about emptiness. That week I read it as a sentence about an account.

The empty account was not idle. It was ready. Everything I would need if a real setup arrived was there: the full size the rule allows, the full room for error, and a clear head that was not managing anything. A forced trade on the fourth morning would have spent some of that on a name the rule did not stand behind, for no better reason than to make the week look less blank.

And there is the other half, the half nobody sees. If the forced trade had gone badly, I would have had a loss to manage, a position to think about, a reason to feel behind. I would have come to the screen on the fifth morning not empty but carrying something. *It blunts the sharp and unties the tangled.* A forced trade is where the tangle starts.

---

The fifth morning was empty too. I wrote the refusals down again. There were fewer of them; the urge was fading. On Friday afternoon I closed the week with nothing in the account and a page of names I had not traded, each with a sentence explaining why not.

I am not going to tell you what those names did the following week, because it does not matter. Some of them probably went up. That is not the test. The test is whether a trade met the rule. None did, so the right number of trades was none.

The page is what I kept. Whenever I have an empty week now, I read it. It is the only record of that week's work, because the work was all refusal. Without the page, the week looks as if nobody was home. With it, the week looks like what it was: a vessel kept empty on purpose, ready to be used.

*How still and clear, as if it were barely there.*
