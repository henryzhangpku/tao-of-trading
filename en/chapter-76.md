# 76. The Living Are Soft

*Part II: The Virtue · 《人之生也柔弱》*

## The Verse

人之生也柔弱，其死也坚强。万物草木之生也柔脆，其死也枯槁。

故坚强者死之徒，柔弱者生之徒。

是以兵强则不胜，木强则兵。强大处下，柔弱处上。

A man is born soft and weak. At his death he is hard and stiff. The ten thousand things, the grasses and the trees, are soft and tender while they live. Dead, they are dry and brittle.

So the hard and stiff are the companions of death. The soft and weak are the companions of life.

So an army that is rigid does not win, and a tree that is rigid meets the axe. The hard and great lie below. The soft and weak rise above.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## The Trader

1. Soft is alive. A position that can still be cut, reduced or closed is a living one. A position that cannot be touched is already dead. Only the price has not been told.

2. Conviction hardens with the loss. The further the price falls, the surer you become. That is the stiffening.

3. To test for it, ask what would make you sell. If nothing comes, you are not holding the trade. It is holding you.

4. Listen for the phrase "on principle." A principle about a price is a refusal dressed as character.

5. Be rigid about the rule and soft about the position. Most traders have it the other way round: rules that change with the week, opinions that cannot be moved.

6. Small size is softness. You can think beside a small position. A large one sets like plaster.

7. The stop is the joint. A body without joints does not stand straighter. It falls in one piece.

8. Grass does not forecast the wind. It has no need to.

9. A rigid army does not win. The trader who must be right about this one cannot be right on average.

10. To be wrong early is to bend. To be wrong late is to break.

## The Story

There is a sentence I used to say with some pride. *I don't sell good things because the price went down.* It sounds like discipline. For most of one year it was the most expensive sentence I owned.

---

This was before I had built anything. No rules in code, no ledger, no forward tests. I had an account, some years of reading, and opinions, which I took for a method. I did real work on what I bought. That is not the embarrassing part. The work on this one was good as far as it went. I had read everything I could find. I had a notebook page with the reasons on it, and the reasons were sound.

I bought it. I had a place in mind where I would get out if I was wrong. In mind: not on paper, and not in the market.

It fell to that place within a few weeks, on nothing. No news. Not one reason on the notebook page had changed. And that, I told myself, was exactly the point. If nothing had changed, then the price was noise, and to sell because of noise would be to let a ticker overrule my own work. I was not that kind of person. I held. On principle.

---

I want to describe what holding on principle is like from the inside, because it does not feel like stubbornness. It feels like integrity.

Each further fall made the case stronger, not weaker: the same good thing, cheaper. I stopped reading the people who disagreed, and I had a reason for that too, which was that they were short-term thinkers. The word I used for the position changed. It had been a trade. Some time in the second month it became a holding. By the third it was something I believed in.

The price was not the only thing that had stopped moving. I had. I could not add, because I was already carrying too much. I could not sell, because of the sentence. I could not take anything else that came along, because the money, and worse, the attention, were all in one place. Every morning I looked at one name first. An account with one frozen position in it is a frozen account, whatever else it holds.

A friend I will call Daniel, who trades far less than I do and thinks more slowly, asked me a question over lunch that spring. He was not arguing. He wanted to understand the position. He asked: what would have to happen for you to sell it?

I started to answer and found there was nothing there. Not a price. Not a date. Not a piece of news. I had built a position with no joint in it anywhere. Whatever happened, the answer was hold. I said something about fundamentals. Daniel nodded and went on with his noodles, and I knew I had just heard myself describe a dead thing.

---

*At his death he is hard and stiff.* The line is not an insult. It is an observation anyone can check. Living things give. A green branch bends under your hand and comes back. A dead branch keeps its shape perfectly, right up to the moment it snaps. If you had to say which of the two branches had principles, you would pick the dead one.

What I had taken for strength was rigor. The position was not strong. It was only unable to move. The cause was not the analysis, which may well have been right; I still do not know, and it no longer matters. The cause was that I had put my conviction in the wrong place. I had made the position the thing that could not bend, when the position is the one part of trading that must.

I sold it a few weeks after that lunch. There was no drama in it, no final plunge, no lesson delivered by the tape. It was an ordinary weekday and an ordinary bad price. What I remember is the next morning. I opened the screen and did not know which name to look at first. It was like getting the use of a hand back.

What principle cost me was not mainly the loss, though there was one, and it was several times the loss I had meant to take at the place I had in mind. It cost me most of a year in which I was not trading. I was standing guard.

---

I did not come out of it less stubborn. I came out with the stubbornness moved.

Everything I build now is rigid in one place and soft everywhere else. The rule is hard: written, frozen, not open to discussion on the day. The position under it is as soft as I can make it. It is small enough that I can think beside it. Its exit is written before its entry. And Daniel's question is answered on the card before anything is bought: *what would have to happen for you to sell it?* If the card has no answer, there is no trade.

Most people, I think, carry it the other way. Their rules are soft and change with the week. Their positions are hard, and they would rather be ruined than wrong. I know because I was one of them, and I had a sentence to prove it.

I do not say the sentence any more. A good thing whose price has fallen through the line I wrote is, for my purposes, a thing I sell. I can buy it again another day. The living can do that.

*The soft and weak are the companions of life.*
