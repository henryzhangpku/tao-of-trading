# 76. The Living Are Soft

*Part II: The Virtue · 《人之生也柔弱》*

## The Verse

人之生也柔弱，其死也坚强。万物草木之生也柔脆，其死也枯槁。

故坚强者死之徒，柔弱者生之徒。

是以兵强则不胜，木强则兵。强大处下，柔弱处上。

A man is born soft and weak. At his death he is hard and stiff. The ten thousand things, the grasses and the trees, are soft and tender while they live. Dead, they are dry and brittle.

So the hard and stiff are the companions of death. The soft and weak are the companions of life.

So an army that is rigid does not win, and a tree that is rigid meets the axe. The hard and great lie below. The soft and weak rise above.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## What You Know

You were raised to be tough, and the market rewards it. No pain, no gain. Weak hands get shaken out; strong hands hold. Once you have done the work and named your level, you commit to it with real size and you do not flinch every time the price wiggles. The market is built to scare people out of good positions, and the trader who bends with every tick ends up with nothing. Professionals are hard. They take the heat, they sit through the noise, they hold on while the amateurs panic. Strength in this business means being the one who does not move.

## The Other Way

1. *A man is born soft and weak. At his death he is hard and stiff.* Watch a trader's first year and his worst year. In the second, he has stopped moving.

2. Stiffness starts in the body. The jaw set, the shoulders up, the hand tight on the mouse. By the time you feel it, the position is already too big.

3. *Grasses and trees are soft and tender while they live.* A small position bends in the wind. A full one has to be defended.

4. "Strong hands" is a phrase made up by people who were holding. The market does not know which hands are strong. It only knows which are large.

5. *The hard and stiff are the companions of death.* The view you will not revise, the level you will not give up, the size you will not cut. Each feels like strength. Each is a branch with no give in it.

6. *The soft and weak are the companions of life.* Soft is not limp. A trader with room in his size can wait, add, cut or leave, and all four doors stay open.

7. *An army that is rigid does not win.* The rigid trader has one move left: hold. Any market can beat a man with one move.

8. *A tree that is rigid meets the axe.* The largest, stiffest position in the account is the one the bad day finds.

9. Soften before the open, not during the trade. Choose the size while your shoulders are down.

10. *The hard and great lie below. The soft and weak rise above.* The trader who looks small and loose is the one still standing when the hard days are over. The soft wins.

## The Story

You did the work last night. You know the level, you know the story, and you know you are right. At the open you buy with full size, because conviction without size is only an opinion. You tell yourself that this time you will not be shaken out.

The first hour is chop. The price drops through your level, climbs back over it, drops through again. Each time it goes under, something in you tightens. You notice your jaw only when it starts to ache. Your shoulders are up near your ears. You are holding the mouse as if someone might take it.

You hold. That is the plan. Strong hands hold. You stop looking at the other names on your screen, because all your attention is needed to keep this one. A setup you would normally take goes by on the next chart and you do not see it. You are not trading any more. You are bracing.

---

Late in the morning the chop resolves the way chop sometimes does, all at once and the wrong way. The price falls through the level and does not come back. You hold through the first leg, on principle, and through the second, because by now cutting would mean admitting the whole morning was a mistake. When you finally sell, it is not a decision. Your body sells. The loss is three times what you would have accepted if anyone had asked you at the open.

In the evening your neck is stiff and your back hurts. That is the trade, still in you.

Look at what the strength was. It was not strength. It was a position too large to bend, held by a man too tight to think. *An army that is rigid does not win.* You had one move, which was to hold, and the market only needed to wait you out.

This is not only a trading lesson. Most of us were told the same thing about life: toughen up, grit your teeth, push through. The hard man, the one who never gives an inch. Laozi looks at the same man and sees something already halfway to dry wood.

---

The other way starts the night before. You still do the work. You still know the level. But you choose the size while you are calm, and you choose one that lets you breathe: small enough that a drop through your level is information, not a threat.

At the open the chop comes again. The price goes under your level, and you feel nothing much. You look at it. It comes back. It goes under again. You let your shoulders down and you ask what the tape is showing, not whether you are right. When it is clear the level is not holding, you cut half. When the next push fails, you cut the rest. It is a small loss, the kind you take without thinking about it at dinner.

Because your hands were loose, your eyes were free. Around midday you see the setup on the next chart, the one you missed the other morning, and you take it.

To anyone watching, you looked weak. You sold the moment it went against you. A trader on the chat would call it weak hands. *The soft and weak rise above.* Weak hands are hands that can still open. A hand that can open can take hold of something else.

---

The old Chinese view turns the hard man's lesson upside down. A green branch bends under snow and springs back. A dead branch is stronger, for a moment, and then it snaps. In a market that will always find out how much you can bear, the stiff trader is the brittle one, and the soft one lasts.

## Try This

Before each entry this week, stop for one breath and check your jaw, your shoulders and your grip on the mouse. If anything is tight, choose a smaller size than you meant to. During the trade, check again whenever the price crosses your level. Watch what changes.
