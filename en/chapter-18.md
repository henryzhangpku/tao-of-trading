# 18. When the Great Way Is Lost

*Part I: The Way · 《大道废》*

## The Verse

大道废，有仁义；智慧出，有大伪；六亲不和，有孝慈；国家昏乱，有忠臣。

When the great way is lost, benevolence and righteousness appear. When cleverness comes forth, great falseness appears. When the six relations are out of harmony, filial piety and kindness appear. When the state is in confusion and disorder, loyal ministers appear.

*The Chinese is the received Wang Bi text. The translation is the author's own.*

## The Trader

1. When a system needs many exceptions, the system is broken. The exceptions are not the repair. They are the symptom.

2. A rule that works does not need a speech in its defence. When you hear yourself explaining why this month does not count, the way is already lost.

3. Watch the length of the rule list. Each line was added for a reason, after a loss. A list that only grows is a record of losses, not a rule.

4. When cleverness comes forth, great falseness appears. The clever patch fits the last bad week perfectly. That fit is the falseness.

5. Exceptions breed. "Except before a report" becomes "except before a report, unless the trend is strong," and soon nobody, including you, can say what the rule does.

6. When the family is out of harmony, piety appears. When the trader is out of step with the rule, discipline becomes a topic. Discipline you have to talk about is already failing.

7. Loyal ministers appear in a state in disorder. Heroic trades appear in a system that is not working. A good system has no heroes.

8. Count the exceptions the way you count the trades. When the count keeps rising, do not add another. Retire the rule, write down why, and start again from the plain version.

9. Retiring a rule is not failing. It is the one exception a good system allows.

## The Story

The rule in this story was retired on a Thursday afternoon, and I want to describe that afternoon, because nothing dramatic happened in it. I did not lose anything that day. I read a file.

---

The rule had begun plainly, the way most of mine do. It bought a certain kind of pullback, sized by how much the market had been moving, and left on a stop or after a fixed number of days. It went through the gate and into a forward book, frozen and pinned by its fingerprint, and for a while it did what plain rules do.

Then came the exceptions.

I say exceptions rather than filters on purpose. A filter is part of a rule from the start and is tested with it. These were different. Each arrived after the rule had done something I did not like, and each was phrased as a carve-out. *Except before a company report*: that one came after a trade was gapped through its stop the morning after earnings. *Except when the broad market is below its own average* came after a run of losses in a falling month. *Except in names just added to an index.* *Except on the day before a long weekend.* And then the ones that had exceptions of their own, which is where it starts to go wrong in earnest: *except before a report, unless the trend is very strong*, because a strong-trend name I had skipped had gone on without me, and I had minded.

Every one of them came with a speech. I know, because I wrote the speeches down. Beside each exception in the changelog there is a paragraph explaining why this case was different, why it was not overfitting, why any sensible person would agree. I was proud of some of those paragraphs. *When the great way is lost, benevolence and righteousness appear.* Reading them now, they are the most righteous prose I have ever written.

---

That Thursday the ledger asked me a question I had been avoiding. I was writing up the season's review of every forward book on the desk, and for each book the review asks one plain thing first: how long has this rule been running under one fingerprint?

For every other book the answer was a season, or nearly. For this rule I had to look it up, and the answer was a few weeks.

It took me a moment to see why. Every exception had changed the rule, and every change had changed the fingerprint. A different fingerprint is a different rule, and a different rule starts its count again at one. The desk is built that way on purpose. So each time I added an exception, the forward test had started over. The rule had been on the desk for much longer than a season, but no version of it had ever lived through a whole one. I had been repairing it so often that it had never once been tested.

I laid the versions out on the screen in order, with their day counts beside them. They looked like a staircase that went nowhere. Each step a few weeks long, each ending in a bad stretch, each followed by an exception that would have prevented exactly that stretch. *When cleverness comes forth, great falseness appears.* Every patch fitted the last bad week perfectly. The fit was the falseness, and the ledger had been counting it the whole time.

---

There were heroes too. That was the other thing the file showed. In two places the changelog recorded a morning when I had stepped in by hand, before any exception existed, to stop the rule doing what it was about to do. I had written those up warmly as well. *When the state is in confusion and disorder, loyal ministers appear.* A good rule does not need saving. If I was saving it by hand, the state was already in disorder.

So I counted the exceptions, the way I count trades. The count was larger than I could have told you from memory, and it was still rising. The reading for this chapter says what to do when the count keeps rising: do not add another. I did not add another.

I retired it.

---

Retiring a rule on the desk is a plain act, and I like that it is plain. The forward book is closed, not deleted. Its record stays in the ledger, every version and every fingerprint, with a line at the end: retired, and why. The why that afternoon was short. *Exceptions outran evidence. No version completed a season.*

Then I went back to the first version, the plain one with no carve-outs, and wrote it into the ledger as a new trial. The same rule as on the first day, but with one more number beside it: the count of everything tried on that family since, exceptions included, so that the bar it has to clear is the honest one. It went into a new forward book with its day count at one. It will have bad weeks. It is not allowed exceptions. If at the end of a full season it does not deserve to run, it will be retired again, plainly, and that will be fine too.

*Retiring a rule is not failing.* I wrote that line in the reading before I had quite earned it. That Thursday I earned it. The failure had been the year of repairs. The retirement was the first honest thing I had done with that rule since the day it was born.
